# 08 - The physical cards: Industrie und Glück, period packs, own-art design cues

Scope note. The coordinator changed the brief mid-run: the game will draw its OWN cards (no third-party art). The
asset-sourcing findings are kept below (section 6) as a fallback and as reference, but the weight of this note is
on what our own faces and back need: index conventions, names/nicknames, back designs, and the piquet/cassino
"Jumbo Index" recipe. Images I downloaded are REFERENCE ONLY.

All claims cite sources I actually fetched this session; ids `[08-Sn]` are listed at the end. "Own reading" =
what I saw in a scan myself, not a published statement.

## Headline findings

- The standard 54-card pack: suits K Q C(avall) J/B; red suits rank K Q C J 1 2 3 4, black suits K Q C J 10 9 8 7; 21 numbered trumps with
  Roman numerals + the unnumbered Sküs. IPCS: "red suits A-4, black suits 7-10 plus 21 horizontally divided trumps ... and the Fool or Skys" [08-S4][08-S6]. de-WP gives the order as "K, D, C, B, 10, 9, 8, 7" / "K, D, C, B, 1, 2, 3, 4" [08-S9].
- Pips and courts have NO corner indices; only trumps carry (Roman) numerals. en-WP: "The pip cards and face cards lack corner indices. All trumps except the unnumbered Excuse use Roman numerals." [08-S10]; de-WP: "Die Zahlen- und Bildkarten haben keine Eckindizes" [08-S9]; WOPC on a c.1905 Piatnik: "square corners and no indices" [08-S7]. So a game that shows corner indices on suit cards is departing from the real pack (sensible for legibility; say so in the tutor).
- The pattern's motto appears on Trump II ("Industrie und Glück" on a rock under a crowned eagle holding sword and sceptre). Earliest known pack: Johann Norbert Hofmann, Vienna, tax stamp 1815, bought by the NÖ Landesbibliothek 20 June 2013 [08-S1]. Piatnik's present Type C goes back to Josef Neumayer (1890; Type B: 1884) [08-S2][08-S3][08-S6].
- Real packs are double-ended: every trump is split horizontally into two different scenes (the lower one upside-down); courts and Sküs are double figures [08-S4][08-S3].
- The low trumps have bird nicknames used for the Königrufen bonuses: Pagat (I, also "Spatz"), Uhu (II), Kakadu (III), Marabu/Quapil (IIII) [08-S16][08-S17]. A sourced oddity: Trump II is called "Uhu" (eagle-owl) although it shows an eagle [08-S2][08-S20].
- Tarock II is the "Uhu", XXI the "Mond" (false Germanisation of mondo = world), the Sküs "Gstieß" in dialect [08-S17][08-S21].
- Piatnik's regional tarock packs are 54 cards, 63 x 113 mm [08-S11]. Backs: "Karo", "Blitz", "Ornament" are current product names [08-S11]; historic backs: "Wiener Halbmond" (black), "Wiener Blitz", blue rocaille on white, blue forest, red/green tartan [08-S3][08-S14][08-S13].
- A complete, museum-scanned Piatnik 54-card pack (Volkskundemuseum Wien, ÖMV/87529, c.1904-12) is marked CC PDM 1.0 with scans up to 2306x4096 px [08-S13]. [REVIEW RA: verified in the saved METS record (vk_mets_201122.xml): all 55 images carry <mods:accessCondition type="use and reproduction"> "CC PDM 1.0" (https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/), scans made by the museum 06.2024; largest files 2314x4101 and 2306x4097, so "up to 2306x4096" is slightly low. The museum's own object record dates the pack 1903-1912 (Witzmann's firm-address argument gives 1904-1912). PDM is a mark asserting no known restriction, not a waiver; the museum gives no separate terms text in the record.] Useful as a REFERENCE even though we draw our own.

## 1. History and iconography of Industrie und Glück

- Origin: the type developed in the Habsburg monarchy from the older Chinesen-Tarock; earliest known examples 1815 Vienna; Reisinger regards 1819-dated packs as "Vorläufer" and speaks of the true type from 1824 (Carl Holdhaus) [08-S9][08-S3].
- Hofmann 1815: copper-engraved, stencil-coloured, backs the doubled "Wiener Halbmond"; 54 cards survive (K Q Reiter Bube + Ace 2 3 4 in hearts/diamonds; K Q R B 10 9 8 7 in spades/clubs; 22 tarocks incl. Sküs); Alscher thinks the original package may have had 78 cards, the 54-card pack being 78 minus the red 5-10 and black A-6 [08-S1]. Alscher notes the 54-card pack is "für Tapptarock (älteste bekannte Spielregel 1821 ...), Königrufen (1827) und Neunzehnerrufen" [08-S1]. Alscher's reading of the motto: a firm slogan of Hofmann, "k. k. priv. Hof-Kartenfabrikant", "Industrie[betrieb] und Glück[spiel]", copied by other makers [08-S1][08-S3]. Others read Industrie = Gewerbefleiß [08-S9].
- Chinesen-Tarock ancestry: Pagat (I) and Sküs, and the turbaned spades king, come from the Chinesen-Tarocke; the earliest Hofmann pack is "halb noch Chinesentarock, halb schon Industrie-und-Glück" [08-S1][08-S2]. The sea-monster figures were replaced first (by the eagle on II) [08-S1].
- The animal-tarot background (what an 1829 table might also have seen): Bavarian Animal Tarot (Göbl, Munich, c.1765; 78 cards, trumps with Roman numerals in a panel top and bottom; Hans Wurst and a fool) [08-S19]; Upper Austrian Animal Tarot (earliest known 1813 Schachner, Wels; Wokaun Prague 1815; Eurich Linz 1817; Dimler Wels 1827; double-ended; 78 cards at first, later 54; "Roman numeral at top left of each illustration") [08-S19]; Tyrol Hunting Tarot (c.1790 to 1882-99, Tyrol, hunting scenes, usually 66 of 78 cards) [08-S19]. Per Alscher's Lower Austrian list: Tier-Tarock Aulich Wiener Neustadt c.1810; Menagerie (von Aken / Martin) 1828-47, later Glanz c.1850; Uffenheimer Walter-Scott tarock 1827 (2nd ed. c.1835, 3rd 1845); Vollnhals Linz 1841 animal tarock; Veduten-Tarock Arnold c.1846 [08-S2]. Tapp-Tarock rules 1821, "Königrufen" 1827 [08-S1].
- Steiger 1829: announcement of a Nationalitätentarock by Johann Georg Steiger: "Auf den zwanzig Tarocks zeigen sich die verschiedenen Nationaltrachten der Stämme des Kaiserstaates in recht ansprechenden Darstellungen" (Wiener Zeitschrift, 12 Dec 1829, p. 1228) [08-S3]. Steiger was the successor of Hofmann's business [08-S3]; Alscher: Hofmann's widow Anna took over in 1822 and Steiger succeeded [08-S2]. 20 motifs of Type A come from it [08-S2]; IPCS: "J. G. Steiger of Vienna ... costumes of the various parts of the Austria-Hungary Empire, the regions being named at the edge of each card" [08-S4]. NOTE for the game: the 1829 date is exactly when the Strohmandel text appeared; Hofmann/Estel/Holdhaus-type Industrie und Glück packs and Chinese-sea-monster, animal and Steiger packs were all in circulation. Packs from before ~1824 would have shown few or no genre scenes.
- Types (Reisinger numbering; Mann/IPCS letters): I (1824-70), II (1850-80), III=A (1859-1936), IV (1859-1914), V=B (1860-today), V/VI (1866-90), VI=C (1890-today), VII (1870-1912) [08-S9][08-S2]. IPCS: A "certainly established by 1865", B c.1865, C c.1890 [08-S4][08-S5][08-S6]. Today B = "southern" (Hungary, Slovenia, Trieste; Piatnik "Kaffeehaus-Tarock" 74x128 mm; Modiano 63x109 mm), C = "northern" (Austria, Czechia, Slovakia, Poland, Romania; Piatnik 63x113 mm) [08-S9].
- Witzmann's museum pack (ÖMV/87529) is Schema 5 (= type B), 54 cards, H 12.5 cm x W 6.8 cm, stencil-coloured lithograph, rocaille back blue on white, made 1904-12 (firm addresses and tax stamp on the Ace of hearts); Piatnik's current version rests on a template by Josef Neumayer of 1884 (year and monogram "JN" on the cards) [08-S3]. The ÖMV scan metadata give the same pack as H 12.6 x B 6.8 cm [08-S13].
- Piatnik: Ferdinand Piatnik (1819-85), born in Ofen, worked for Anton Moser's card-painting shop, married the widow in 1843; "& Söhne" from 1882 [08-S3]; WOPC: "Ferd. Piatnik & Söhne A.G." from 1917 [08-S7].
- Card sizes (all measured by the cited source): current Piatnik 54-card tarock 63 x 113 mm (Karo, Blitz, Ornament) [08-S11]; "Ladies' Tarock" (Type A) 52 x 97 mm vs 63x113, Schafkopf 56x100 [08-S10]; Union (Vienna, c.1934, type V) 78 x 121 mm [08-S14]; Viennese 52-card Piatnik c.1870 (not a tarock) 67x101 [08-S14].
- Variants: Piatnik's Tarock Nr. 71 (1920s/1937) for Droggn, 66 cards, a more colourful, kitschier redraw of type B (goat on trump XVI replaced by a roe deer and fawn) [08-S9]. Czech inter-war: trump II with a bare-headed falcon and "Audaces Fortuna Juvat" (Česká grafická Unie) [08-S6]; Hungarian packs: "Szerencse Fel!" for the motto, Lwów: "Przemysl i Szczescie", a c.1930 Denk pack: "Dem Vaterland die Treue" [08-S4][08-S5].

## 2. What the trumps show (iconography)

Official/printed statements:
- Mostly rural genre scenes and regional costume, "different at each end" [08-S7][08-S9]. Witzmann: Biedermeier "Menschen in ihren regionalen Trachten ... aber auch märchenhaft-orientalische Genreszenen"; later figures "in einer operettenartigen Kostümierung ... bühnenhaft"; some women "eher einem Balletteusentarock entnommen", others recall rococo shepherdesses [08-S3]. Source images: Trachten prints, e.g. de Moleville "Costume of the Hereditary States of the House of Austria" (London 1804) [08-S3].
- I (Pagat): Harlequin figure (harp-playing Harlekin with Columbine and tambourine, from the Chinesen-Tarock) [08-S3]. Own reading of Type C scan: a harlequin with a long staff striding, the other end a fan or umbrella-like figure [08-S23].
- II ("Uhu"): crowned eagle with sceptre and sword on a rock, inscription "Industrie und Glück"; other end: the Tyrolean cloth/carpet merchant striding towards the Hofburg, in Type B/C past the Archduke Charles equestrian monument (Witzmann) or the Prince Eugene monument (Alscher's Type C table) [08-S3][08-S15]. The Tyrolean comes from Steiger's 1845 Berufe-Tarock [08-S2]; the motif seems to come from Viennese street-cry (Kaufruf) prints and Mandlbogen [08-S3].
- XXI ("Mond"): Type C shows a crescent over an oriental scene with a sultan; Type B an older similar scene without moon [08-S10]. Own reading: sultan seated with attendants and a woman, crescent in the sky [08-S23]. Type A: a Kirchweihfest (church fair) from Steiger 1845 [08-S2][08-S15 table].
- Sküs (XXII, unnumbered): "der große Harlekin ..., der den kleineren auf der Spitze seines Hutes in seiner Hand balancieren lässt" (a large harlequin balancing a small one on his hat), only the small one's topknot still recalls its Chinese origin [08-S3]. Sküs and Pagat are "grafisch verschieden dargestellte Harlekine mit Halskrause und Krummsäbel" from the Chinese tarots [08-S9]. Own reading: the Sküs is a double-ended full-card harlequin; no numeral [08-S23].
- Regional labels on trumps in Alscher's Type C/Type A tables (places and peoples, e.g. Böhmen, Krainer, Tiroler, Istrier, Mährer, Schlesier, Hanaken, Lombarden, Croaten, Steirer, Ober-/Inner-/Unter-Österreicher) [08-S15][08-S2]. IPCS Type A: II couple dancing to a musician (Siebenbürger), IIII man on a galloping horse (Ungarn), IX man with staff and girl with posy (Krainer), X man seated with stick (Salzburger), XII woman threatening seated man (Unter-Österreicher), XIV woman spinning (Unter-Österreicher), XVIII man serenading seated girl (Italiener), XX man leaning on gun, woman seated (Istrier) [08-S4].
- Courts: kings with crowns; a spades king in profile/turban in some versions; the Type B/C Cavall (knight) on horse; Witzmann/Darquenne-style packs have a trade address printed on the jack of clubs/diamonds [08-S3][08-S7]. Pagat.com: "the king has a crown, the knight has a horse, the queen is female and the jack is the other one" [08-S16].

HUNCH followed: "genre scenes - any jokes?" Findings: (a) the eagle on II is nicknamed Uhu [08-S2][08-S20]. (b) Alscher's Hofmann note records a "Fehlersuchbild" joke: the double-ended cards are not mirror images (e.g. the hearts queen has a necklace at one end but not the other; the small Chinese harlequin stands slightly differently) [08-S1]. (c) Witzmann's "operettenartig" costumes are the nearest to a published joke. I found no period feuilleton jokes about the pictures; open lead.

## 3. Index and corner conventions (for our own faces)

- Suit cards: no indices on the traditional pack [08-S9][08-S10][08-S7]. Pips are just the suit symbol repeated; "Ace" of the red suits is a single pip and is called "1" (Red 1 2 3 4), black has no ace in the 54-card pack [08-S4][08-S16][08-S9]. Own reading of ÖMV scan: red pips are plain red hearts/diamonds, black pips plain black spades/clubs, no numerals, tax stamp on the heart "1" [08-S13 images].
- Trump numerals: large black Roman numerals, set into the top-left of the upper picture and, rotated 180 degrees, bottom-right of the lower picture (own reading of Alscher's Type C scan and of the ÖMV scan; the Modiano Type B photo of II shows the same) [08-S23][08-S12]. WOPC: "large Roman numerals" [08-S7]. IPCS on the Upper Austrian animal tarot: "bearing roman numeral at top left of each illustration" [08-S19]. [REVIEW RA: confirmed by looking at the ÖMV 87529 scan: trump 4 reads IIII (four bars), 18 reads XVIII, II shows the crowned eagle on the "Industrie und Glück" stone, Sküs is an unnumbered double harlequin. Note also that the court cards (e.g. king of diamonds) carry a small suit pip in the top-left corner, so "no corner indices" is true for letters/numerals only.] Alscher's tables give IIII, not IV, for 4: the table prints "IIII" [08-S15]; the Hofmann/Type-A tables use "IIII" [08-S2]; scan numerals read III, IIII, V ... (own reading) [08-S13 images]. USE "IIII" if period-faithful; "IV" otherwise.
- Sküs: no numeral at all [08-S10]; de-WP: "unnumbered Sküs" [08-S9]. Some old/Czech texts number it XXII (Alscher's tables label it XXII) [08-S2][08-S15].
- Colours (own reading): numerals black on cream paper; scenes stencil-coloured in red, ochre, green, blue; red suits red, black suits black [08-S13 images][08-S23].
- 54-card breakdown for our own deck: 4 x 8 = 32 suit cards + 22 trumps (21 + Sküs) [08-S4].

## 4. Names and nicknames (period German and English)

Trumps:
- I = Pagat (also Pagatl, Spatz = sparrow; from Italian bagatto; "le petit" in French). [08-S17][08-S20]. Pagat-ultimo = last trick with the I [08-S16]. (The form "Pagatl" is NOT in a source I fetched [UNVERIFIED].)
- II = Uhu ("Uhu" = eagle-owl) [08-S2][08-S20][08-S21]; scoring: win the 2nd last trick with II [08-S16].
- III = Kakadu (cockatoo); alternatives "Pelikan, Kanari, Maus" [08-S16].
- IIII = Quapil (pagat.com "Quapil", elsewhere "Marabu"); alternatives "Wildsau or Marabu" [08-S16]. pagat.com's table names IIII "Marabu" in the main bonus list but "Quapil" in the notes, so both are in use [08-S16]. en-WP Strohmandeln table: Kakadu = Tarock III, Quapil = IV [08-S24].
- The low trumps as a group: "the birds" (die Vögel) [08-S16].
- Some groups give bonuses for V and VI [08-S16].
- XXI = Mond ("moon", false Germanisation of il mondo/le monde = world) [08-S17]; Hungarian "huszonegy" [08-S20]. Capturing it with the Sküs = Mondfang [08-S16][08-S17].
- XXII/unnumbered = Sküs (from French l'excuse), dialect Gstieß / Stieß; Hungarian skíz [08-S17][08-S20]; Viennese "Gstieß" [08-S3][08-S21]. Capturing it = Sküsfang [08-S16]. In Baden Cego dialect: "Alt", "Stieß" [08-S17].
- Trull (Pagat + Mond + Sküs) from French tous les trois; Alscher notes elsewhere "les sept tarots" [08-S17][08-S2].
- Other names for XX, XVIII, etc.: NONE found in the sources fetched. The regional pictures have regional labels only in Steiger-type packs [08-S4].
Courts: Industrie-und-Glück courts are King, Queen (Dame), Cavall/Reiter (cavalier/knight), Bube (jack) [08-S9]; de-WP lists "Bube, Reiter oder Cavall, Dame, König" [08-S9]; the Hungarian-made packs use the same; Alscher's Hofmann text: "König, Dame, Reiter, Bube" [08-S1]. The abbreviation set K D C B (de) is given by de-WP [08-S9]; English: K Q C J [08-S16]. "Valet" as a name for the jack: not found in the Austrian sources fetched.
Points (for card values on the tutor): kings 5, Sküs/XXI/I 5, queens 4, knights 3, jacks 2, others 1 [08-S16].

## 5. Backs (cue for our plain back)

- Current Piatnik regional tarock packs: "Tarockkarten Karo" (item 193613), "Tarockkarten Blitz" (193415), "Tarockkarten Ornament" (193514); each 54 cards, 63x113 mm, folding box; the pages do not describe the back designs (the names are the designs) [08-S11]. de-WP: "Je nach dem grafischen Muster der Rückseite unterscheidet man ... 'Roter Blitz', 'Ornament', 'Karo', 'Jagdtarock' usw." [08-S20].
- Historic: Wiener Halbmond (doubled on Hofmann 1815; black on Piatnik c.1870 and Titze & Schinkay c.1885) [08-S1][08-S14]; Wiener Blitz (woodcut) [08-S3]; blue rocaille pattern on white (Piatnik 1904-12) [08-S3]; blue forest motif (Ladies Tarock) [08-S13]; Ladies' Tarock back with a capercaillie on a tree [08-S3]; Union c.1934 red-and-green tartan [08-S14]. Own reading of the ÖMV back scan: a blue-on-white symmetrical rocaille / floral ornament in a rounded panel [08-S13 images].
- Suggested plain back: a two-colour symmetrical ornament, blue or red on cream; Blitz = zig-zag; Karo = lozenge lattice. (Own suggestion.)

## 7. What the earlier games used, and the Jumbo Index recipe

- Piquet (CREDITS.md): faces from "Public domain complete playing card deck" by AustinGabriel64 (Commons, 27 July 2026, CC0); back "Reverso baraja española" by Germarquezm (CC BY-SA 3.0, adapted: adaptation also CC BY-SA 3.0); large-text faces are the project's own design (user's recipe, 1 Oct 2026), drawn in the browser (`web3d/src/faces.js`) with the device's bold UI font and symbol font, nothing bundled [08-S18]. Cassino and bezique reuse the same faces and back; cassino adds twelve courts cut from a c.1760 Sarton-family Spanish pack (Cary Collection scan via WOPC; public domain) [08-S18].
- Recipe, commit d2507ac (1 Oct 2026, "The user's recipe for Jumbo Index card faces, as given"): Canvas 2D; card 7 x 9.8 units (5:7); rank in `-apple-system, system-ui, sans-serif` weight 700, size 2.2, cap-top at y=0.3; suit glyph via `"Apple Symbols","Segoe UI Symbol","Noto Sans Symbols 2"` size 2.8 placed by measured ink so the top is below the rank and its centre on the rank's axis; U+FE0E variation selector prevents emoji hearts/diamonds; the same corner repeated rotated 180 degrees; paper `#fbfaf6`, ink `#15171c`, red `#c8202c` [08-S18]. Later commits (7f92ebd, aa7e5cc, 2 Oct): the index is fitted to the strip of each card that a fanned hand shows (strip 1.46 units, margin 0.25), "make the font on each card just big enough to be seen in that space"; the 10 may run on under the next card; `isPhone` = coarse pointer and short side under 600 px; Settings "Large Text (Optimized for smaller screens)" defaults on phones [08-S18].
- Adapting it to tarock: (1) trumps need Roman numerals up to XXI (up to five characters, "XVIII"), so the "10 runs under next card" trick will not do; fit the numeral width to the strip, or add a bold nickname beneath; (2) four-suit pips are 1-4 and 7-10, courts K Q C J; (3) the Sküs needs a non-numeral mark (star, harlequin glyph or "Sküs"); (4) trumps need a distinct colour from suits, e.g. a cream or tinted ground; (5) a fanned hand of 6 to 12 cards. Nicknames: I Pagat, II Uhu, III Kakadu, IIII Quapil, XXI Mond, Sküs [08-S16][08-S17].
