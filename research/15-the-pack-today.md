# 15 - The pack today: why "tarot" is not fortune-telling here, and the C card

Scope: the two things a new player finds strange, for the tutorial to draw on. (1) The word and the cards: English speakers
know "tarot" as a fortune-telling deck; Tarock is the card-game family the deck was made for. (2) The C: the Cavall, a mounted
knight between queen and jack, which the 52-card pack most players know does not have. Last, where this game's own faces
depart from a real pack. Ids `[15-Sn]` are listed at the end; saved copies are in `research/sources/15/` (not in git) or in the
subdirectory named. [read] = text read in this session; [OCR] = scanned book, OCR read; [secondary] = encyclopedia or
promotional page; [UNVERIFIED] = reported by a source I read, not seen myself.

## Headline findings

- Tarot packs were made for card games: the earliest documented packs are Italian, 1440-50 (Milan, Ferrara, Florence,
  Bologna) [15-S2]; Ferrara court accounts of 1442 name "carte da trionfi" [15-S1, ch. 4]. The cards were first called
  *trionfi*; "tarocchi" first appears c.1502 [15-S2]. English "trump" comes from *trionfi* [15-S2][15-S7].
- Fortune-telling with the tarot is later and French. Dummett found no mention of cartomancy with the tarot, or of any occult
  meaning, before 1781 [15-S1, p. 100]. It begins with Antoine Court de Gébelin's essay in *Le Monde primitif*, vol. 8 (1781)
  [15-S1, ch. 6][15-S6]; Etteilla (Jean-Baptiste Alliette) published the first tarot divination method in 1783 and the first
  deck made for it in 1789 [15-S1, p. 107][15-S6]. Fortune-telling with ordinary playing cards was already established
  in the 18th century; the tarot was taken up after it [15-S1, p. 100]. The occult tarot stayed in France for about a century
  and reached other countries at the end of the 19th [15-S1, p. 100]. The Egyptian origin de Gébelin proposed had no
  evidence behind it [15-S6].
- Both uses are still in circulation: en-WP: "two distinct types of tarot packs in circulation: those used for card games and
  those used for divination"; some game patterns (Tarot de Marseille) are also read for divination [15-S2]. pagat.com: "In the
  twentieth century the occult interpretation of the cards has become more popular and widespread than the games for which
  the cards were originally intended" [15-S9].
- Tarock is alive in Austria. pagat.com: Tarock cards are "commoner in the east"; Königrufen is "by far the most popular"
  Tarock game there; "Strohmandeln (2 players)" is among those "less widespread" [15-S10]. de-WP: in France and Austria
  Tarock "ist nach wie vor populär" [15-S7]. Piatnik (Vienna) lists "Tarockkarten Karo, 1 x 54 Blatt in Faltetui, 63 x 113 mm,
  Artikelnummer 193613" (checked 15 Oct 2026) [15-S11]; its pattern is the Industrie-und-Glück Type C (Piatnik, from
  1890) [08-S6][08-S2]; en-WP calls Type C "the standard" [15-S3].
- Spelling: Tarock is the German spelling and the game's name in Austria; German keeps "Tarot" for the esoteric deck, while
  English and French use one word for both [15-S7]. en-WP: "The word 'tarot' and German Tarock derive from the Italian
  Tarocchi" [15-S2]. In German the trumps themselves are also called "Tarock" [15-S7].
- The Cavall: the Tarock pack has four courts, the French-suited pack has three. Werner, *Das moderne Tarokspiel*: the Tarok card "differs
  from the French by the addition of the Cavall, a male figure on horseback" [15-S12, pp. 7-8].
- Value: the cavall counts 3, between queen 4 and jack 2 [15-S14][15-S13, § XI][RULES 1.7].

## 1. The cards were made for games

- Dummett: "the Tarot pack was not invented for use in fortune-telling, but as an instrument of play, and was used only for
  the latter purpose throughout the fifteenth, sixteenth and seventeenth centuries" [15-S1, p. 100] [OCR]. The same chapter
  records that the 1659 *Maison des jeux* describes games with the tarot pack and "making no mention of cartomancy" [15-S1,
  p. 100]. Dummett's 1980 preface reports the common belief he met ("the pack was well known to have been invented for the
  purpose of fortune-telling") and p. 3: "Almost everyone would be likely to say, 'It's used for fortune-telling, isn't it?', which
  is true as far as it goes, but ... conveys an impression at variance with the true history of the cards" [15-S1, pp. 3, Preface].
- The pack: Italian suits (swords, batons, cups, coins), ten numeral cards and four courts per suit, 21 numbered trumps,
  and the unnumbered Fool; Dummett calls the 21 "triumphs" and says the occult names (Magician, High Priestess,
  Major and Minor Arcana) are "historically inaccurate" [15-S1, p. 3]. The word "arcana" belongs to the occult
  use [15-S8].
- Games: tarot games spread over most of Europe except the British Isles, Iberia and the Balkans [15-S2]; the 18th century was their
  greatest revival [15-S2]. Games are still played in Austria, Czechia, Denmark, France, Germany, Hungary, Italy,
  Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia and Switzerland [15-S9]. Tarot "was the one of earliest games to introduce the concept of
  trumps"; virtually all later trump games inherited the idea from it [15-S9].
- In the Austrian games the trumps are "Tarock": 22 trumps, the Sküs (which "looks a little like a joker") and 21 cards with
  large Roman numerals XXI to I [15-S10]. The Fool plays as the highest trump (effectively the 22) in the Central European
  games [15-S9]. pagat.com on the Sküs: "looking a bit like a joker, though there is no historical connection" [15-S9].
  In Königrufen the second-highest trump XXI is the Mond and the lowest, I, the Pagat [15-S14].

## 2. When and by whom the occult reading began

- Court de Gébelin: a Protestant pastor and Freemason [15-S1, ch. 6]; his chapter "Du Jeu des Tarots" (*Le Monde primitif*, vol. 8, Paris 1781) read the trumps as Egyptian priestly
  wisdom, with no historical evidence; an essay by the Comte de Mellet printed with it tied the 22 trumps to Hebrew letters
  and first spoke of the Book of Thoth and Egyptian fortune-telling [15-S6][15-S1, ch. 6].
- Etteilla (Alliette), a Paris seed merchant turned cartomancer: first book on fortune-telling with ordinary (piquet) cards
  1770; from 1783 *Manière de se récréer avec le jeu de cartes nommées tarots*, a method for the tarot, prompted by de
  Gébelin's volume; in 1788-89 a deck designed for divination, "the first deck of cards specifically designed for occult
  purposes" [15-S6][15-S1, pp. 106-107].
- After that the occult tradition spread: Dummett, p. 100: confined to France "for an entire century", to other countries
  only at the end of the 19th [15-S1]. en-WP: the custom decks "began after French occultists made elaborate, but unsubstantiated,
  claims about their history and meaning in the late 18th century" [15-S2]. The 78-card Tarot de Marseille, a game pack, is
  also used for divination [15-S2].
- de-WP: until the end of the 18th century the development of the Tarot-Karten and the Tarock cards is identical; afterwards
  sets made explicitly for divination took on symbolic-esoteric content [15-S8].

## 3. Naming: Tarock, Tarot, tarocks

- Tarock (G.) = Tarot (E., F.): same word, from Italian *tarocchi*; the word's own origin is uncertain; *taroch* was a late-15th/
  16th-century Italian word for foolishness [15-S2]. en-WP disambiguation: "Tarock is German for Tarot" [15-S5a].
- In German usage the esoteric deck is "Tarot", the games and cards "Tarock"; "Viele Sprachen haben denselben Begriff für
  Tarock und Tarot, insbesondere das Englische und Französische" [15-S7][15-S8].
- "Tarocks" in play are the trumps (German: "Tarock"); the nicknames (Pagat I, Uhu II, Kakadu III, Quapil
  IIII, Mond XXI, Sküs) are in `08` §4 [08-S16][08-S17].

## 4. The C card (Cavall, Reiter)

- Packs with a knight: the second court of Italian and Spanish packs is "a mounted Cavalier or Knight" with the jack below
  him (it. *cavallo*, sp. *caballo*, French *cavalier*, G. *Reiter* or *Kavall*) [15-S1, pp. 6-7]. The Italian and Spanish
  packs have no queen; the knight takes the queen's place [15-S4]. Tarot packs, Italian- or French-suited, have king, queen,
  knight, jack [15-S1, p. 3][15-S2]. Dummett: the presence of the knight "is not distinctive to the Tarot pack" [15-S1, pp. 6-7].
- Packs with three courts: "A court consisting of King, Queen, and Jack (in French, Roi, Dame and Valet) is found only in the
  French-suited pack" [15-S1, p. 6]; en-WP: "knights were dropped in favour of queens in non-tarot French decks" [15-S4]. The
  52-card deck most players know is that three-court French-suited pack [15-S5].
- German and Swiss packs have Ober and Unter (no knight), except the Württemberg pattern where the Obers ride horses,
  copied from Cego tarot packs [15-S1, p. 6][15-S4]. Origin: the Mamluk deck's second court officers became the knight and
  the knave in southern Europe [15-S4].
- The Austrian 54-card pack: each suit has "nicht nur drei, sondern vier Hofkarten: den Buben, den Reiter oder Cavall, die
  Dame und den König" [15-S15 = 08-S9]; black suits K D C B 10 9 8 7, red K D C B 1 2 3 4 [08-S9]; pagat.com calls the card
  "Rider" in the Austria survey and "knight" in the Königrufen rules [15-S10][15-S14]; Identification: "the king has a
  crown, the knight has a horse, the queen is female and the jack is the other one" [15-S14].
- Names in period Austrian sources: "Cavall" in a Vienna Tarock book of 1756 ("der Cavall für 3") [15-S13] [OCR]; in Werner's
  *Das moderne Tarokspiel* (Hartleben, Vienna): "ein König, eine Dame, ein Cavall, ein Bube" and "durch das Hinzukommen des
  Cavall, einer männlichen Figur zu Pferde" [15-S12] [OCR]; Herder 1854/1857 "Caball, Caval, Roß, im Tarock eine Karte,
  Reiter und Roß darstellend, der sog. Ober" [05b, LEX-S31]; a Fremdwörterbuch of 1878 glosses *Cavall* "Ritter im Tarokspiel; Springer im Schachspiel" [05d S7].
  A "Cavallerie" is a declared set of court cards of one suit in the same period texts [05d].
- Letters: de-WP gives the Austrian order as K, D, C, B (König, Dame, Cavall, Bube) [08-S9]; the English C stands for Cavall
  or Cavalier; pagat.com spells ranks out and prints no letters [15-S14]. RULES 1.2 uses K Q C J for the English pack and the
  UI switches to K D C B in German (`web3d/src/tarockfaces.js`, `COURT_LABELS`). C is also the letter English players read as
  clubs; the suit symbol beside it settles it [D: the project's face design].
- Counting values: kings 5, queens 4, knights 3, jacks 2, others 1 (Sküs, XXI, I 5) [15-S14]; the 1756 book gives 5, 4, 3, 2 for
  king, queen, cavall and jack [15-S13].

## 5. Where this game's own faces depart from a real pack

Checked against `docs/RULES.md` 1.2, `web3d/src/tarockfaces.js` and the Decided list in `PLAN.md` (14 Oct 2026):

| Feature | Real Industrie-und-Glück pack | This game |
|---|---|---|
| Suit-card indices | none: "The Zahlen- und Bildkarten haben keine Eckindizes" [08-S9]; "pip cards and face cards lack corner indices" [08-S10]; WOPC c.1905 Piatnik "no indices" [08-S7]. The courts do carry a small suit pip in the corner [08 review note on the ÖMV scan, 08-S13]. Dummett: indices only became usual in the late 19th century and some standard patterns still omit them [15-S1, p. 6] | a corner index (rank over suit) on every suit card, large in "Large Text" [`tarockfaces.js`] |
| Court cards | figures (king crowned, knight on a horse, queen, jack), double-ended [08-S3][15-S14] | letters K Q C J (K D C B in German) on a plain ground, no figure |
| Numeral cards | pips of the suit, no numerals [08-S13 scan] | pips in Classic, rank numerals in Large Text |
| Trumps | genre scenes at each end, large Roman numerals top left and (turned) bottom right, 4 written IIII [08-S3][08-S15] | tinted field with the numeral top left and (turned) bottom right, 4 written IIII, others IX XIV XIX as on the real pack |
| Nicknames | not printed; Pagat, Uhu, Kakadu, Quapil, Mond, Sküs are table names [08-S16][08-S17]; I found no pack description that lists printed nicknames | printed on I, II, III, IIII, XXI |
| Sküs | unnumbered double-ended harlequin; some Czech texts number it XXII [08-S3][08-S15] | a star and "SKÜS" |
| Pack | 54 cards, 63 x 113 mm (Piatnik Type C), patterned back [15-S11][08-S11] | 54 cards; plain back (PLAN, Decided 14 Oct 2026) |

So the tutor can say: the large indices and the letters are for reading at a glance; a real pack has pictures, not letters, on its
courts and no index on its suit cards.

## For the tutor

Each line is at most 25 words, English then German, with its citation. Voice: the kibitzer's, dry and kindly (`docs/TABLE3D.md` 6.5).

1. **Made to play.** These cards were made to play with, in Italy, by 1442. Fortune-telling found them only in 1781, in France. [15-S1, ch. 4 and p. 100][15-S6]
   - Diese Karten wurden zum Spielen gemacht, in Italien, spätestens 1442. Das Wahrsagen kam erst 1781 dazu, aus Frankreich.
2. **Still played.** Nothing here tells your fortune. If I may: Austrians still play Tarock, and Piatnik of Vienna still prints this pack. [15-S10][15-S11]
   - Hier wird kein Schicksal gedeutet. Wenn ich mir erlauben darf: In Österreich spielt man Tarock noch immer, und Piatnik in Wien druckt dieses Blatt weiter.
3. **The tarocks.** The tarocks are simply trumps: I to XXI, and the Sküs above them all. The word "trump" comes from their old name, trionfi. [15-S10][15-S9][15-S2]
   - Die Tarocke sind schlicht Trümpfe: I bis XXI, und der Sküs über allen. Das Wort „Trumpf“ stammt von ihrem alten Namen, trionfi.
4. **Tarock, tarot.** In German the game is Tarock and the fortune-teller's deck is Tarot. English uses one word for both, hence the confusion. [15-S7][15-S8]
   - Im Deutschen heißt das Spiel Tarock und das Blatt der Wahrsagerin Tarot. Das Englische hat für beides ein Wort, daher die Verwechslung.
5. **The C.** The C is the Cavall, a knight on horseback, worth 3. Tarock packs kept him; the 52-card pack dropped him. [15-S12][15-S1, p. 6][15-S14]
   - Das C ist der Cavall, ein Reiter zu Pferd, 3 Points wert. Das Tarockblatt hat ihn behalten, das Blatt zu 52 hat ihn verloren.
6. **Our faces.** On a real pack the courts carry pictures, not letters, and suit cards no index. These big letters are for a quick look. [08-S9][08-S10][15-S1, p. 6]
   - Auf einem echten Blatt tragen die Bilder keine Buchstaben und die Farbkarten keinen Index. Unsere großen Buchstaben sind für den raschen Blick.

## Gaps / leads

- Ferrara 1442 is from Dummett's OCR; the primary registers were not seen. Earlier dates (Martiano da Tortona's lost pack, 1418-25) are in en-WP only [15-S2].
- Decker, Depaulis and Dummett, *A Wicked Pack of Cards* (1996), the standard account of the occult tarot, was not read; the dates in section 2 rest on Dummett 1980 and en-WP.
- Court de Gébelin's birth year differs between sources (1719 in Dummett, 1725 in en-WP); not used.
- No source read prints the letters K Q C J or K D C B as a stated convention on real cards; de-WP lists the order with those letters. Real Austrian packs show no letters on courts (08), so the letter is the project's own.
- Whether any real pack prints the trump nicknames: no description read says so.
