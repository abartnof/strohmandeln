# 12 - Follow-up chaser: the nine open items

Research strand 12. Files saved in `research/sources/12/` (see "Sources"). Page images were read by eye where marked `[IMG]`; OCR text normalised by me is marked `[OCR]`; secondary quotations are marked `[2nd]`. Translations are mine. Long-s normalised.

## Headline findings

1. **Alvensleben 1853 transcribed (item 4)**, from the page images (pp. 576-577). It is *not* a reprint of Haas's rule text with "Haas" credited: it prints the Strohmandeln paragraph and, just before it, an advertisement for Karl Haas's Tarok-Tappen manual. The Strohmandeln text is the same as Haas 1829/1834/1842 (03-S4..S7) except "Erfindung" for "Mißgeburt" in the Tatteln sneer. [12-S1]
2. **The "jejune ... much better and quite different" wording is Dummett's own judgment (pp. 457-458), not a paraphrase of Ulmann pp. 244-245 (item 3).** Dummett says only that the later game "is described in both of Ulmann's books" and that "these various descriptions agree very closely". en.wikipedia's footnote to "Ulmann (1890), pp. 244-245" for "jejune" is therefore probably a misattribution of the wording [REVIEW RF: softened from "is a misattribution"; section 3 itself says Ulmann's pages are unread, so what Ulmann says there cannot be checked]. Dummett also dates *Das Buch der Familienspiele* "about 1895" and "before 1901", not 1890. No scan of Ulmann's book was found. [12-S2]
3. **Werner's *Das moderne Tarokspiel*: 2nd edition appeared in 1891, by early June (item 6).** First edition announced Oct/Nov 1883. [12-S6..S9]
4. **The Tepl "Erster europäischer Tarockcongreß" took place on Sunday 15 Jan 1893 (item 5).** The original report (Neuigkeits-Welt-Blatt, 24 Jan 1893, p. 9) is read in full: it prints no Strohmandl rules. The "unified rules" were house rules for kibitzers, card-money and a charity kitty. Lovely banter material (see section 5). [12-S10..S12]
5. **Item 9: on the Volkskundemuseum pack, only the 4 is additive.** IIII, IX, XIV, XIX, XVIII, XVII. Trump II is a crowned eagle with a sword standing upright beside it [REVIEW RF: was "holding a sword"; on the contact sheet the sword is not in the claws], perched on a stone lettered "INDUSTRIE UND GLÜCK". Numerals sit top-left of the upper picture and bottom-right (rotated) of the lower picture. The Sküs has no numeral. Contact sheets saved. [12-S13]
6. **harbour-tarock is GPL-3.0-or-later (item 8).** Its Strohmandeln spec is a 2864-line German document with a flag for every rule ambiguity (list below) and a detailed AI design. [12-S14]
7. **Torberg's actual book text could not be read (item 1)**; the archive.org copy is lending-restricted. The wording is available only as quoted by de.wikipedia (XVII and XVIII, "Ross!"/"Topschin!", "sonst galt's nicht"). New: Mayr/Sedlaczek's own 2002 web text says Rostopschin was "recht beliebt" between the wars, "vermutlich vor allem im Vierer-Tarock" and "in Torbergs Tante Jolesch ausführlich beschrieben", and gives XVII/XVIII. So XVII+XVI (de.wp glossary) looks like the outlier. [12-S3..S5]
8. **Item 2 (Freud) is still second-hand.** The "Samstag abends ... Kartenspielen" line is quoted by Sedlaczek/Mayr (UNESCO Gutachten) and an ORF page, both without date or edition. The "Behagen in der Unkultur" remark is reported only by Sedlaczek/Mayr, with no source. [12-S15, S16]
9. **Item 7: the 1839 "zweite verbesserte und vermehrte Auflage" exists** (HathiTrust catalogue record, Illinois copy, full view; Google Books id JClFAQAAMAAJ), but I could not open its page 121 (HathiTrust is behind Cloudflare, Google Books quota-blocked). [12-S17]

## 1. Torberg, *Die Tante Jolesch*, "Am Kartentisch", p. 217

- Archive.org holds two items: `dietantejolescho00torb` (Langen-Müller 1975) and `dieerbendertante0000torb`. Both are `access-restricted-item`; `_djvu.txt` downloads redirect (302) to "Item not available" and the in-book search (`fulltext/inside.php`) returns the same page. I did not try to get round the lending restriction. Strand 09 saw 401s for the same reason. [12-S3 metadata]
- The only wording obtainable is de.wikipedia (Königrufen), citing "Die Tante Jolesch, Langen Müller 1975, Seite 217" [2nd]: "Die im Café Central beheimateten Meisterspieler hatten eine unglaublich komplizierte Abart des ohnehin anspruchsvollen „Königrufens" erfunden, die sie „Rostopschin" nannten. Hier gab es außer dem „Pagat Ultimo" ... noch einen „Uhu pre-ultimo" ... Eine noch höhere Prämie erzielte, wer im Verlauf der Partie mit den Tarockwerten XVII und XVIII zwei Stiche hintereinander machte. Das war nicht im voraus anzusagen, sondern erst beim Ausspielen, und zwar bei der ersten Karte mit „Ross!" und bei der zweiten mit „Topschin!", sonst galt's nicht." [12-S4, saved `sources/02b/wp_koenigrufen_de.txt`, line 279]
  - Answer to the question: **XVII and XVIII** (not XVI), "Ross!" on the first, "Topschin!" on the second; not announced beforehand. The Café Central is named for the master players. Note the passage is about *Königrufen* (a four-hand game); the Uhu "pre-ultimo" here is the second-lowest trump in the second-to-last trick.
  - The same de.wp article elsewhere has the glossary line "Rostopschin: Eine alte Prämie - hintereinander zwei Stiche mit XVII und XVI machen." [12-S4 line 746]. The two lines sit in one article and disagree; the Torberg quotation (a quotation of a printed text) should win over the unsourced glossary line. I could not check Torberg himself, so the quote is [2nd] and the Wikipedia editor's transcription could carry the error either way.
- **New second source on the trumps:** Mayr & Sedlaczek's own web text (Wayback snapshot of 29 Jan 2002): "Hier geht es darum, mit Tarock XVII und Tarock XVIII zwei Stiche hintereinander zu machen, wobei man beim Spielen von Tarock XVII Ross! sagt und beim Spielen von Tarock XVIII Topschin!" and "Der Rostopschin ist eine alte Tarockregel, die schon in der Zwischenkriegszeit in Wien recht beliebt war, vermutlich vor allem im Vierer-Tarock. Sie wird in Friedrich Torbergs Die Tante Jolesch ... ausführlich beschrieben." [12-S5] This is a primary rules text for the Strohmandl menu, and it also contains the "Mondopfer" and "II und III aufheben" advice (also quoted in harbour's spec).
- Sedlaczek's claim that Torberg misapplied the name to the whole game: still known only from de.wp ("Mayr/Sedlaczek, Das große Tarockbuch, Seite 255f") [2nd]. The 2002 web text above is consistent with it (calls it a bonus, "vermutlich vor allem im Vierer-Tarock").
- Searches for quotations in derstandard / wienerzeitung / Sedlaczek's books found nothing further (WebSearch returned only the Wikipedias; diepresse.com is not reachable by the search tool). The Sedlaczek/Mayr UNESCO Gutachten mentions Torberg only to say Tarock "galt nicht als klassisches jüdisches Spiel" [12-S16].
- Unresolved: the exact book wording. Lead: a library copy or the 2011 Anaconda edition; The Beck/dtv reading sample (`beckassets.blob.core.windows.net/.../9783423012669_excerpt_005.pdf`) was returned by search but I did not fetch it (probably only the table of contents; the search result confirms "Am Kartentisch" is a chapter title).

Another Freud card-playing letter exists to Fliess (1900, Tarock on Saturday evenings), per a search summary, not verified here.
- **"Lassen Sie mir doch das bisschen Behagen in der Unkultur."** Reported by Sedlaczek & Mayr (same Gutachten) as Freud's reply when partners complained about worn cards in the Berggasse, "eine Anspielung auf sein Werk 'Das Unbehagen in der Kultur'" [2nd] [12-S16]. ## 3. Ulmann, *Das Buch der Familienspiele*, pp. 244-245

- **Not found online.** archive.org metadata and full-text searches for the title: no hit. de.wikipedia (Tatteln) cites the book (A. Hartleben, Wien/München/Pest, "1890", pp. 187-189) with no scan link [12-S18]. Wienbibliothek digital search returns only old library catalogues. ZVAB lists a Hartleben edition "o.J." / "um 1900" [search result only].
- **What Dummett actually says** (pp. 457-458, the pagination header "458 Part IV" falls after the passage) [OCR] [12-S2]:
  - Early form: "This description is reproduced in other works that give Tarok-Tappen in its early form, the Encyclopädie der Spiele of von Alvensleben and that of Friedrich Anton, and Stabenow's book of 1908-10." (cf. item 4: confirmed for Alvensleben.)
  - "This rather jejune game was ousted by a much better and quite different one, also called Strohmandeln, Strohmandl, Strohmann-Tarock or, in Hungary, Strohmandli. It does not seem ever to have been known in Germany, but only in Austria and Hungary, and must date from the second half of the nineteenth century, the 1880s at the latest, but perhaps much earlier ... It is described in both of Ulmann's books, and also by Bermann, Werner and Unger; modern accounts are to be found in the books by Beck, Löw and Babsch, in that by Dr Widder Lajos mentioned above, and in the Piatnik leaflet. Fortunately, these various descriptions agree very closely."
  - So "jejune" and "much better and quite different" are **Dummett's words about the 1829-form game**, and "very attractive ... both challenging and very exciting" attributed to Ulmann by en.wp is not in Dummett's passage. Whether Ulmann says it at pp. 244-245 can only be checked on the book. The en.wp footnote "Ulmann (1890), pp. 244-245" probably comes from a different reading of Ulmann; I cannot confirm.
  - Dummett's dates (p. ~441-442 notes, `p4.txt` lines 1540-1560): *Das Buch der Familienspiele* "(Vienna, about 1895)"; it "was published later than the first edition of the same author's Tarokbuch, for which it carries an advertisement; but on internal evidence, it must have been published before 1901, since it refers to the 'present century', meaning the nineteenth." That conflicts with Wikipedia's 1890.
  - Dummett on the early form: "The winner of each trick takes the top card of the dummy hand, shows it to his opponent and adds it to his tricks." (Alvensleben/Haas say only "wirft sich ... zu".) He dates the earliest form as 1829 and the 1889-1890s one as "c. 1890 to the present day" [12-S2].

## 4. Alvensleben, *Encyklopädie der Spiele* (1853): the Strohmandeln passage

Source: Alvensleben, L. von, *Encyklopädie der Spiele*, Leipzig: O. Wigand, 1853, pp. 576-577, section "Tapp-Tarok (Dreiern)" [IMG, read from the BSB scan on archive.org `10431553bsb`, leaves 601-602 = printed pp. 576-577; page images saved]. The archive.org OCR cuts the line ends; the transcription below is from the images. Long-s normalised.

Immediately before it (p. 576, end of the Tapp-Tarok section), an advertisement: "Den Freunden dieses, jetzt fast allgemein verbreiteten Spiels zeigen wir hiermit an, daß eine weit umfassendere Behandlung desselben in der Karl Haas'schen Buchhandlung in Wien unter dem Titel: „Theoretisch-praktische Anleitung zur gründlichen Erlernung des beliebten Tarok-Tappenspieles, sowohl durch genaue Bestimmung aller Regeln und Feinheiten, als auch durch die Beobachtung und Auseinandersetzung mehrerer angeführter sehr schwieriger Beispiele," bereits in einer zweiten, viel verbesserten und mit einem Anhange über das König- oder Tarokrufen vermehrten Auflage erschienen ist."

Then (pp. 576-577):

> **Tarok-Tappen unter zwei Personen oder das sogenannte Strohmandeln.**
> Dieses Spiel wird ganz so, wie das gewöhnliche Tappen unter drei Personen gespielt, auch werden die Karten wie für drei Theilnehmer ausgegeben, nachdem die obersten sechs Blätter als Kauftalon gelegt sind. — Die dritte fehlende Person ist der Strohmann, dessen Karten verdeckt in derselben Reihenfolge, wie sie gegeben wurden, liegen bleiben. Während nun die andern beiden mitsammen spielen, wirft sich derjenige, welchem der Stich gehört, allemal das oberste Blatt von den Karten des Strohmanns zu, das jedoch, wäre es auch der Skis, nicht sticht, sondern nur dem Empfänger des Stiches zu dessen Vortheil zählt. Schmiert demnach der Strohmann zufälliger Weise dem Spieler viel Könige und Figuren, indeß dem Gegner auf seine Stiche nur Taroks oder leere Blätter fallen, so ist das Spiel leicht zu gewinnen. — Indeß da hierbei weit weniger die Geschicklichkeit als der blinde Zufall wirkt, so möchte diese Unterhaltung wohl nur von Leuten gewählt werden, denen das edle Piquetspiel gänzlich unbekannt ist, zumal, da es in Einseitigkeit noch das veraltete Mariage und jene neuere Erfindung „Tatteln" benannt, bei weitem übertrifft.

Translation: "This game is played exactly like ordinary Tappen among three; the cards are also dealt as for three participants, after the top six cards have been laid down as the buying-talon. The third, missing person is the straw man, whose cards stay face down in the order in which they were dealt. While the other two now play together, whoever wins the trick always throws himself the top card of the straw man's cards, which, even if it were the Skis, does not win the trick but only counts to the advantage of the trick's recipient. If the straw man by chance 'smears' many kings and court cards to the player while only trumps or blanks fall to the opponent on his tricks, the game is easily won. But since chance, far less than skill, is at work here, this pastime would probably be chosen only by people to whom the noble game of Piquet is entirely unknown, especially as it surpasses in one-sidedness even the antiquated Mariage and that newer invention called 'Tatteln'."

Remarks:
- Same text as Haas 1829 p. 94 / 1834 / 1842 p. 121 (per 03) with one visible variation: 03 records "Mißgeburt Tatteln" in Haas; Alvensleben prints "Erfindung". Alvensleben's 1853 text therefore stands in the Haas line, but I did not collate the whole paragraph word-for-word against the scans in 03.
- The Piquet sneer, the "blinde Zufall", and "Schmiert ... viel Könige" are all in this 1853 reprint. This also means the sneer is in print at least from 1829 to 1853 (03) and the "1839" attribution on en.wp is a spot citation.
- The dealing here: six cards laid aside as "Kauftalon", then 3 x 16 cards (48), the straw-man hand is the "third person". The rules of bidding and the buying are those of Tapp-Tarok (Dreier) on pp. 573-576, which I did not transcribe.
- There is no Strohmandeln chapter in Alvensleben's table of contents beyond this one (the contents line in the BSB OCR, line 805, reads "Strohmandeln . 9 376" [OCR]; the printed page is 576, so the OCR misread the 5).
- Alvensleben's work is a Wigand compilation; Friedrich Anton later edited it (5th ed. 1889; see Tatteln wikitext) [12-S18, 12-S2].

## 5. The 1893 Tepl "Erster europäischer Tarockcongreß"

Established by strand 11 (Brünner Morgenpost 2 Feb 1893). My findings:
- **Prager Tagblatt, 2 Feb 1893, p. 6** [snippet via ANNO]: "Erster europäischer Tarotkcongretz [sic, OCR]. Aus Tepl wird dem 'Fremdenblatt' geschrieben: Vorige Woche fand hier der 'Erste europäische Tarockcongreß' statt ..." (same text as the Brünn copy). The Südsteirische Post, 8 Feb 1893, p. 6 has the same text. [12-S10]
- **The original report: Neuigkeits-Welt-Blatt (Vienna), Tuesday 24 Jan 1893, 3. Bogen, p. 9, "Ein 'Tarock-Kongreß'. (Original-Bericht des 'Neuigkeits-Welt-Blatt')."** [IMG, full text read from page crops, saved] [12-S11]. Facts:
  - It took place "am 15. d. M. Abends" (15 Jan 1893, a Sunday) in the decorated rooms of Herr Roth, on the invitation of the catechist of the Tepl Volks- und Bürgerschule, P. Egidius Ranwolf (the image reads Ranwolf; the OCR has Ranwolf/Ranwolf variants). About 300 people "(Tarockspieler und 'Kibitze')". The "Gemeinde Kruman" came in festive dress with its "Büttel" and a standard; a "Hoveraner Musikkapelle" played the entry march.
  - Telegrams of greeting from "Tepler Tarockirern" in Vienna (Wieden, Gasthaus "zum goldenen Sieb"), Theusing, Einsiedl, Stift Tepl, Marienbad and "den 64 Tarockirern von Auschowitz" [REVIEW RF: the page image reads Auschowitz (Úšovice, by Marienbad); "Anschowitz" was an OCR reading] were read.
  - Ranwolf was elected president by acclamation, on the motion of the Dechant P. Ambros Winkler; his talk covered "die Entstehung des Kartenspieles". The committee's motions "wurden sämmtlich - mit nur geringen Abänderungen - angenommen". The only motions the report details concern **kibitzers and money**: "Ein sehr interessantes Kapitel über 'Kibitze'" with two classes, **"Laute Kibitze"** and **"Stumme Kibitze"**, who buy "Jahreskarten" (50 kr and 25 kr), non-subscribers pay 1 or 2 kr per evening into a "**Kuckuck**" (a box) placed in every inn, which also receives the fines "für eventuelle Verstöße gegen die aufgestellten Regeln"; **Kartengeld** for the host: "für eine neue Karte mit 3 kr., für eine überspielte Karte mit 1 kr. per Mann und Abend"; proceeds go at Christmas to clothes and shoes for poor schoolchildren. Officers: a "Direktor" (Adalbert Selig), "Professoren" of the Tarock game, and a "Supplent" (substitute) [REVIEW RF: image reads "Supplenten"; "Suppplent" was a typo].
  - Then an "official Tarockspiel" of one hour "nach den vorher beschlossenen Regeln": 28 tables ("Partien"), the rest as kibitzers for lack of room; **in that hour "22 Valat und 54 Pagat ultimo gespielt, welch' erstere mit einem 'Tusch', letztere mit einem Hornsignal markirt wurden"**, with a "respektables Erträgniß für den 'Kuckuck'". Play went on "nach Mitternacht".
  - The game is never named; "Tarockspiel" generally. No word "Strohmandl", "Tapper" or "Königrufen" in this version, and no rules text. The Brünn/Prague wording ("einheitliche Spielregeln für 'Strohmandl', 'Tapper', 'Königsrufer' etc. festgestellt, die allgemein veröffentlicht werden sollen") is an editor's compression and the only place the three game names appear. The report with the names comes from the Fremdenblatt (another wording). [12-S10, S11]
- **Follow-up: Neuigkeits-Welt-Blatt, 28 Dec 1893, p. 10, "Der erste europäische Tarok-Kongreß"**: the first annual meeting on Sunday 17 Dec 1893 in Roth's rooms, "die erste Jahres-Versammlung des am 15. Jänner 1893 ... stattgehabten 'ersten europäischen Tarok-Kongreß'"; the president says the "Beschlüsse" and "Satzungen" were "meist strikte durchgeführt" over the year, and honours the late "Tarokprofessor" Anton Hohler. Accounts follow (Kibitzkarten 14 fl 75, etc.). A "Kongreß-Allee" and "Kongreß-Bäume" were planted in memory (snippet) [12-S12]. I did not find an ANNO hit for a separately printed set of "Tarokregeln" in 1893. The terms searched were: Tarockcongreß, Tarokcongreß, Tarok-Congreß, Tarockkongreß/-kongress, Tarock-Congreß, Tarokkongress, europäischer Tarockcongreß, Tarotkcongretz, Tepl Tarock, Ranwolf, Tarockregeln, Tarokregeln, Tarok-Regeln. Hits: 1893-01-24 (NWB), 1893-02-02 (Prager Tagblatt), 1893-02-08 (Südsteirische Post), 1893-12-28 (NWB), a 1904 Nordböhmisches Volksblatt back-reference to "den berühmten Tarockkongreß, der vor einigen Jahren in demselben Saale stattfand" (a different town; not checked), 1925 items (not 1893). Conclusion: **no published Strohmandl rules from the congress located.**
- Bonus for banter and tutor tone (kindness rule respected): "Laute Kibitze / Stumme Kibitze" (loud and silent kibitzers); the "Kuckuck" kitty for fines; "Tusch" for a Valat and a horn signal for a Pagat ultimo; "Tarokprofessor" and "Supplent". 19th-century and in period voice.

- The Strohmandeln spec (`docs/strohmandeln.md`, 2864 lines, dated 12 Sep 2026, profile default `AT-STROH-MS-ERW`) says it relies on Mayr/Sedlaczek's web text ("[MS-WEB]", Wayback of 29 Jan 2002, `tarockspiele.com/strohmann.html`, which I fetched and saved: [12-S5]), a translation on wareh.org, the 2007 Wiener Zeitung piece, a Tirol club rule sheet (tarock.tirol, Jan 2025) and Wikipedia's accounts of Beck, Furr, Bamberger and Dummett. It states that it did **not** read Beck, Bamberger, Dummett, Ulmann or the 1829/1839 texts in full and that pagat.com has no Strohmandeln page (404). So it adds no primary evidence for the period rules: its value is the list of ambiguities, the Tirol profile and the Mayr/Sedlaczek tactics (also on the 2002 page).
- **Rule interpretations it adopts, ambiguity by ambiguity (defaults; "flag" = switchable):**
  1. Timing of laying the straw men: dealt before bidding (`strawmen_dealt_before_bidding = true`); Beck/de.wp deal them after someone takes up (§2.4).
  2. Throw-in (re-deal) if a hand has no tarock before the first reveal: yes, same dealer (`throw_in_no_tarock = true`; Tirol sheet silent) (§2.5).
  3. Re-deal if both pass: only for the simple game (`redeal_if_both_pass = simple_game`); who deals again: same dealer in the default, forehand deals in the Beck profile (`redeal_dealer`) (§3.2, open question 11).
  4. First lead: always forehand (`first_lead = forehand`); Furr: the taker leads (§9.3).
  5. Reveal: taking tarocks/kings at once or setting them aside first is cosmetic (`open_cards_taken_immediately = true`) (§4.2).
  6. **The last card of a pile**: goes to the hand unseen (`last_card_is_secret = true`; Tirol and de.wp/Beck say not shown, M&S silent); alternatively it lies face-down on the table but plays as a hand card (`last_card_stays_faceDown_on_table`, cosmetic) (§4.5). The whole information model hangs on the first flag.
  7. Face-up straw-man cards belong to the hand for the purposes of following suit, trumping and discarding ("Deckblätter gehören zum Blatt"); face-down cards never count, so a king can "sleep" under a Dame (§4.4, §6.3).
  8. No discarding or talon exchange; no skart (§4.6, a negative finding the spec flags as its own inference).
  9. Draw (35:35 or 35⅓:34⅔) in the simple game only; in a taken-up game the taker loses (`draw_scope = simple_only`); a draw makes the next round count double ("Nullrunde"), non-stacking (§1.7.1, §8.3).
  10. Win threshold 36 out of 70 (counting in threes, "35 Punkte und 2 Blatt") (§1.7).
  11. Value of the simple game: 1 plus a Grammel point at 45+ in the default; 2 in the classic/2007 profile (never mixed); Beck 1/2/3 as en.wp tabulates, noting the two Wikipedias contradict each other on Beck (§9.2, §9.4).
  12. **Grand Point ("Grammel-Punkt")**: 45 for the winner, opponent 25 or less, using rounded points (p3 >= 134, not 135); Furr's variant uses 44 (§9.6).
  13. Bonuses all "silent" (counted in tricks, not announced) in the default; classic profile: trull/four kings announced in the hand; Tirol: silent 1, announced 2 (§5.2).
  14. Capture bonuses: a Pagat caught in the last trick scores for the opponent, also for Uhu/Kakadu (`pagat_capture_bonus`, `bird_capture_bonus = true`) (§9.5).
  15. Uhu, Kakadu on, Quapil off by default (on in the 2007 profile); Mondfang unprized by default (§5.4, §5.8).
  16. **Rostopschin** = tricks with XVII then XVIII, calls "Ross!"/"Topschin!", spelling a flag ("Ross" for M&S, "Ros" for Furr); XVII/XVIII, not VII/VIII as en.wp's table says (verified there against Furr p. 111) (§5.5, §9.9). The call is constitutive: omitting it cannot lose the bonus; the first trick may be won by trumping with XVII.
  17. Whether face-up cards count as "in the hand" for "trull in the hand": yes (`bonus_hand_includes_faceup = true`), flagged as an open question (§9.7).
  18. Kontra off by default (M&S advise against it); if on it doubles only the game value, not the bonuses (§5.10, §9.8).
  19. Trischaken when both pass (Tirol profile only), with "Stichzwang" read as "must overtrump when able" and Pagat as last tarock (provisional) (§5.9).
  20. Misdeal penalty 1 point, 2 in a doubled round (§2.6); match to 11 points ("race11") in the Tirol profile (§8.2).
  21. Hungarian "Strohmandli" (42 cards, 3 piles of 3, no bidding) is treated as a separate game, not implemented (§9.12).
- **AI design (to borrow as ideas only):** perfect-information Monte Carlo with determinisation, and the key point that the AI must sample its **own** hidden pile cards too (§10.1, §10.8). Void inference expires at the next reveal of that player's piles (§10.1). Take-up heuristic from M&S: "sichere Stecher" worth about 20-25 points plus 6-7 tarocks, with fractional counts (king/trull card 4⅓, Dame 3⅓, Cavall 2⅓) and thresholds 23⅓/21⅔/20⅓ by level (§10.2). Eleven tactical rules H1-H11 from M&S: play your own piles off fast; stop the opponent playing his; never be left with an empty hand and full piles; play a lying Dame at once ("in 4 von 10 Fällen" the king sleeps in a pile); play the higher of two lying cards; serve from the piles when both are only blanks; don't sit on a pile in a void suit; the Cavall feint (not more than twice a game) (§10.3); "nach hinten spielen" (§10.4); lead from the longest closed trump run (§10.5); Pagat hunt only with 11+ trumps (§10.6); keep II and III, Mondopfer (§10.7). Three levels: beginner (rule heuristics, 15% random slips), normal (inference, counting), hard (PIMC 40 worlds + alpha-beta, exact from trick 18) with a 250 ms per-move budget (§10.8). Evaluation function is a weighted sum (§10.9).
- The spec's learning-mode chapter (§11), glossary and three practice hands (§11.7) are worth a read for the tutor, again as ideas only.
- Observations: the spec's statements about our other open items are second-hand (Wikipedia, Furr) and it repeats the "1839 p. 121" and "Ulmann 1890" citations without having seen the books. It does cite Furr p. 111 directly and I could confirm the XVII/XVIII point from M&S's own page (12-S5).

## 9. Volkskundemuseum Wien pack ÖMV/87529 (Industrie und Glück)

- **Record** (OAI-METS record 201122 from `sammlung.volkskundemuseum.at`, saved copy [12-S13]): label "ÖMV/87529: Tarockkartensatz 'Industrie und Glück'", urn:nbn:at:at-vkw-28892; 55 digitised images (54 cards plus the back), each tagged accessCondition "CC PDM 1.0" (`creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/`); literature note "Witzmann, Nora: ›Industrie und Glück‹ - ein Tarockspiel aus Wien. In: Österreichische Zeitschrift für Volkskunde LXX/2016, S. 95-110, hier S. 95 und Abb. 1-6." A University of Innsbruck newsletter (neuerDings, search result) says the pack came to the museum in 2015 from a Viennese household, is a Piatnik lithograph, stencil-coloured, "Schema 5" (not fetched; strand 08 has the dating c. 1904-12 from the card's tax stamp). Strand 08 already downloaded the 55 images (1000 px, `sources/08/volkskunde_87529/`); I made two contact sheets from them (`sources/12/omv/sheet_a.jpg` = images 01-12, `sheet_b.jpg` = images 13-24) and checked the numerals by eye [IMG]. Caveat: the sibling record 201120 (ÖMV/75849) carries a title field that names 87529 and vice versa; the 55 images come from 201122 whose logical div is labelled 87529, so I treat them as 87529.
- **Numerals [IMG]** (trump by image number: 01 = Sküs/Gstieß, 02 = XXI ... 22 = I):
  - **4 = "IIII"** (additive, as expected), shown as four thick strokes.
  - **9 = "IX"**, **14 = "XIV"**, **19 = "XIX"**, **18 = "XVIII"**, **13 = "XIII"**, **8 = "VIII"**, **17 = "XVII"**, **16 = "XVI"**, **6 = "VI"**, **7 = "VII"**. So only the 4 is written additively; the others are subtractive where the Roman convention is (IX, XIV, XIX) and "XVIII" is the standard form. No VIIII/XIIII/XVIIII. This confirms RA's note in the review and gives the numeral style for the game: IIII, otherwise standard.
  - The numerals are large black slab-serif capitals. **Position: top-left of the upper picture and, rotated 180 degrees, bottom-right of the lower picture** (the cards are double-ended, a different picture in each half). The Sküs (image 01) is a harlequin pair and carries **no numeral**. Suit cards (images 23ff; I looked at the K and D of diamonds) have a large suit pip at top-left and bottom-right; no letter index on the courts.
- **Trump II is an eagle (image 21):** a crowned eagle, wings half open, with a sword upright beside it, perching on a stone bearing "INDUSTRIE UND GLÜCK." with flowers around. So the "Uhu" (owl) nickname for trump II is a mismatch with the picture, on this pack at least: the tutor joke is valid ("the Uhu is an eagle in disguise"). Strand 05 says Uhu/Kakadu/Quapil are 20th-century names (so a 19th-century voice should not use "Uhu"); RA also notes Witzmann and Alscher have "Adler auf Tarock II (heute Uhu genannt)".
- Other things the sheets show: trump I is the harlequin ("Pagat") with a harp, and trump III-XX are genre scenes (III: couple before a house; IIII: groom and horse; V: couple at a well; VI: harbour; VII: peddlers; VIII: shepherdess; IX: man and donkey; X: couple on a terrace; XI: woman with goat; XII: couple; XIII: woman with a calf or goat; XIV: grape harvest; XV: country couple with hens; XVI: couple at a garden wall; XVII: couple with pigs; XVIII: kneeling man and woman with a washing tub; XIX: two men in oriental dress; XX: pair at a pillar; XXI: dancing couple). Descriptions are my reading of the scans, not museum text.
