## Headline findings (LIT)

1. **The Kiebitz (onlooker) is the main character of Viennese card-table humour, and he comes with a "law": "§ 13: Kiebitz, halt's Maul!"** (Kibitzer, shut your trap). It is called "one of the main paragraphs" as early as 1895 [LIT-S11] [REVIEW RD: the 1895 text says only "Hauptparagraphen"; the number "13" is first found in the saved snippets in Kikeriki 9 Sep 1897 (q_kiebitzhalt.txt), then Illustrierte Kronen Zeitung 12 Jul 1912, Der Tag 15 Oct 1934 etc. So "§ 13" as a name is attested from 1897 in this note's material, and the 1895 article is only the earlier "law" without the number], printed on placards hung in inns [LIT-S44, LIT-S40], and quoted in court [LIT-S43]. It is often extended: "Kiebitz, halt das Maul und zahl'!" (… and pay) [LIT-S41]. **Design hunch:** in a two-player game the tutor can be a period Kiebitz, and "§ 13" can be the button that mutes him.
2. **Nestroy (1835) already gives a full Tarock complaint-song**, the earliest literary card banter found here: in *Zu ebener Erde und erster Stock* the servant Johann sings about playing Tarock "von halb drei bis um neune" (from half past two until nine), players who play "dirty" or badly, players who pass holding a hand full of trumps, a player who looks into his neighbour's cards, and finally a great hand "and then it's: misdeal" ("'s is vergeb'n") [LIT-S1]. [REVIEW RD: (1) the original text said "four, five and six hours" and "a kibitzer"; the song says neither. (2) The scene is in Act II (Zweiter Aufzug), not Act 1. (3) The wording quoted in this note is that of the 1890 Chiavacci/Ganghofer edition, which is an edited text; the Projekt Gutenberg-DE text of the play (https://www.projekt-gutenberg.org/nestroy/ebenerde/ebenerde.html, Act II, Auftritte 21 and 28-32; re-fetched by RD) reads differently in places, notably "Der denkt sich: »Pagat ansag'n? Wird's ratsam sein?«" and "Endlich steig'n tous les trois auf; jetzt heißt's, 's is vergeb'n" where the 1890 text has "Wird ein Ultimo z'machen wohl sein?" and "Endlich hat man die Trull". So "Trull" in this song is the 1890 editors' word, not (as far as the PG text shows) Nestroy's; "tous les trois" is. Use the PG wording as the primary-closer text.] The same play stages a game with calls in it: "Ich bin Erster und spiele", "Tous les trois … vier Könige … Pagat Ultimo!", "Ich passe!", "Solo!" [LIT-S1].
3. **Strohmandl is real in the feuilletons and is talked about as the poor relation of Tarock.** Two people play it when the third man is missing: "Wie schade, daß wir keinen 'Dritten' zu einem 'Tapper' haben! Können Sie nicht 'Strohmandeln'? — Zu dienen." (Figaro, 1888) [LIT-S15]. A 1931 joke has two men waiting two hours at a Strohmandl game "for the third" [LIT-S14]. In 1910 the summer exodus means "the Königrufer sinks to a Tapper, the Tapper to a Strohmandl" [LIT-S63]. A 1908 parliamentary joke supplies a Strohmandl close-count proverb: "35 und 2 Blatt; das ist eigentlich der stolzeste Sieg" [LIT-S16].
4. **Two rich, fully read Tarock dialogues** give dozens of verbatim lines: Julius Löwy's "Der Giftnik'l" (Illustrirtes Wiener Extrablatt, 1903) [LIT-S9] and Alexander Ranzenhofer's "Königrufen" (Pilsner Tagblatt, 1917) [LIT-S8]. Between them: the slow player ("Sie legen ja so langsam wie eine alte Henne die Eier!"), the bad deal ("Das halbe Spiel liegt ja unterm Tisch!"), the lucky opponent ("Ein Schwein hat der Mensch", "den Seinen gibt's der Herr im Schlafe"), the jinx ("Wann der kibitzt, weicht m'r der Sküß aus wie der Teufel aner Kirchen"), the Mondfang hummed to a song ("Guter Mond, du gehst so stille"), the Pagat as "der Spatz" ("jetzt kommt der Spatz unters Messer!"), "Ein guter Misch ist das halbe Spiel", and "Nur noch eine Runde".
5. **Slow play has its own stock gibe: "Eine Karte oder ein Scheit Holz"** (a card or a log of firewood), plus "Wecken S' mich auf, wenn der Herr dort drüben ausspielt!" (1924) [LIT-S10].
6. **"Versehen ist verspielt"** (a slip is a play, i.e. a card played in error stands) is attested as the card players' basic rule in 1892–1916, in a Tarock question column and in a 1916 Tarock court case [LIT-S37, LIT-S38, LIT-S39]. It makes a good, kind "no take-backs" line.
7. **Surprise: suit-name puns used as cheating signals.** Jörgel Briefe (1910) reports a Landstraße café where a partner asks for Pique with "Die Karten picken ordentli'", for Karo with "karrierte Hosen", for Treff with "der Mensch is nie z' treffen", for Herz with "so ein herzig's Kind" [LIT-S19]. These make good, harmless table puns, as long as the computer never uses them to signal.
8. **Vocabulary turned up in passing:** *Gstieß* for Sküs, in Viennese street speech (1926–34) [LIT-S54]; *Spatz* for Pagat (1911, 1917) [LIT-S57, LIT-S8]; *Skatindeln / Skartindeln / Statiedln* for low discards (1898, 1910, 1917) [LIT-S28, LIT-S19, LIT-S8]; *packeln* (stacking the trumps when gathering the cards) and *die ganze Pletsch'n* (the whole bunch) [LIT-S19]; *g'spritzt* (doubled) [LIT-S31, LIT-S18]; *Gstüsmarterer* (card-torturers) [LIT-S78]; *Patzer / Mordspatzer / Erzpatzer* (bungler) [LIT-S9, LIT-S8].
9. **Not found:** Torberg's *Tante Jolesch* has a chapter "Am Kartentisch" (p. 73 in the dtv edition, per its table of contents) [LIT-S67b], but no text of it could be fetched, so nothing is quoted. Polgar, Friedell, Kraus, Altenberg, Kisch, Raimund, Anzengruber and Chiavacci yielded nothing about Tarock in the texts that could be fetched (see Gaps).

## Other findings worth passing on

- Freud's card-table quip, "Lassen Sie mir doch das bisschen Behagen in der Unkultur" (on playing Tarock with worn cards), and Handke's line that Tarock lets you "die Farben zeigen, sie zugeben und, vor allem, lakonisch sein" (show your colours, follow suit and, above all, be laconic) are reported in Sedlaczek & Mayr's UNESCO expertise (2024) [LIT-S67]. Both are secondary quotations of Königrufen players; Freud's is said to refer to his worn cards.
- Strohmandl's social profile in the papers: the game of retired men in the Kaffeehaus at midday (Kronen Zeitung, 12 Dec 1916), of a doctor and a "Rechtspraktikant" in the café (1895, 1905), of two players on trains (Prager Tagblatt 1930; Der Morgen 1926: 24 hours non-stop from Vienna to Nice), and of married couples at home (1882, 1912, 1923) [snippets in `anno/q_strohmandl.txt`]. Court cases (1895 Café de l'Europe; 1905; 1937, where a defendant claims "Strohmandl ist kein Hasard, sondern ein Kunstspiel, man muß kombinieren") show it was played for real money and counted as a legal game of skill [LIT-S11; Kleine Volks-Zeitung 16 Jan 1937, p. 11, OCR snippet]. This belongs to the cultural strand; flagged here for the coordinator.
- Rule-talk embedded in the press (for the rules strand): "Beim 'Strohmandl' muß der Aufnehmer, um zu gewinnen, mindestens zwei leere Blätter über 36 haben" (Prager Tagblatt, 24 Sep 1920, p. 8); "beim Strohmandl gewinnt man mit 36 Points, als solche zählen aber auch 36 und 2 Blatt" (NWT, 16 Oct 1911, p. 25); "Strohmandl mit 40 Karten" (NWT, 6 Dec 1934, p. 22) [REVIEW RD: the page image (03/anno/nwg19341206_p22.jpg) clearly reads "40 Karten"; "48" was the snippet OCR error]. All [OCR snippet], in `anno/q_strohmandl.txt`.

## Hunches followed (LIT)

- **Nestroy, by grepping all of the 1890–91 *Gesammelte Werke***: this yielded the best early source (the 1835 Tarock song and card scene), plus couplets in *Einen Jux*, *Unverhofft* and *Der Erbschleicher*. No Strohmandl or Sküs anywhere in Nestroy's OCR.
- **The ANNO REST API, and IIIF crops of whole columns**: the most productive method. It turned short hits into fully read feuilletons (Löwy 1903, Ranzenhofer 1917, the 1924 kibitz sketch, the 1895 Strohmandl court case).
- **"Kiebitz" as the tutor persona**: strongly supported. The kibitzer is the period's comic figure *par excellence*, and there is a ready-made "law" (§ 13) to serve as a mute button.
- **"Versehen ist verspielt" as a card proverb**: confirmed (1892–1916) and explicitly applied to Tarock.
- **A play "Sküs, Mond und Pagat" / "Tarockerl"** [REVIEW RD: the statement below "No text found" is wrong. A printed text exists: Ferdinand Rosenau, *Scüs, Mond und Pagat: komisches Zauberspiel als musikalisches Quodlibet in zwey Aufzügen*, Wien 1821, on archive.org (scsmondundpaga00rose), saved and quoted by 05d (`sources/05/poly/ia_scsmondundpaga00rose_djvu.txt`); RD verified the quoted lines there (e.g. "Unnüße Mühe, der Scüs sticht ja den Mond", "Ich habe den Pagat Ultimo gemacht!", characters Herr Dreyer, Herr Solo, Herr Ultimo, Tarokerl). That it is the same piece as the 1819 Gläser setting is likely (same title, same Quodlibet genre) but not proved]: a "komisches Zauberspiel" with music by Franz Gläser (*Der Sammler*, 1 Jan 1819, p. 8; staged in Brünn 1821; the *Wiener Zeitschrift* of 24 Mar 1836 reports a benefit revival of the "long-vanished" *Tarockerl, oder Sküs, Mond und Pagat*; Pest/Ofen 1839 per Bäuerles Theaterzeitung) [OCR snippets in `q_skues.txt`]. No text found on archive.org or in Google Books. **Lead not closed.** [REVIEW RD: superseded: see 05d S4, the 1821 printed libretto IS on archive.org.]
- **Pagat as "Spatz"** (sparrow): found twice (1911, 1917). It is a nice nickname for the AI to use.
- **Torberg**: blocked (copyright; no full text could be fetched). Only the existence of the chapter "Am Kartentisch" is confirmed.
- **Gutenberg sweep** of Altenberg (3 volumes), Schnitzler (4 texts), Raimund (8 plays), Anzengruber, Chiavacci (*Weltuntergang*), Bahr, Kisch (2), Rosegger (several), Saar, Ebner-Eschenbach: Tarock found only in Saar, Ebner-Eschenbach, Schnitzler (*Die griechische Tänzerin*, one passing mention: "Am Sonntag spielte Karl oben manchmal Tarock …", no dialogue) and Rosegger (passing mentions).
