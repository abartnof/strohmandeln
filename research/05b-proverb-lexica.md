# 05-LEX fragment: lexica, proverb dictionaries, etymologies, early attestations

Scope: Grimm DWB, Wander's *Deutsches Sprichwörter-Lexikon*, Meyers, Adelung, Campe, Pfeifer (DWDS), Schmeller, *Idioticon Austriacum* (1824), Deutsches Textarchiv (DTA) full-text hits. Hügel 1873 and Schranka 1905 were left to the parent. Everything was fetched on 2026-10-14 and saved under `research/sources/05/lex/`. Fraktur long-s is normalised. DTA texts that print "ſs" come out as "ss" after conversion, so single "s" is restored in quotes. Ids are `LEX-Sn`.

Notes on the sources:
- **Wander volume numbers.** The woerterbuchnetz API gives a "book" number that does not always match Wander's printed volume. Where a quote was confirmed by a DTA hit on the printed volume, the volume and year are given ("DTA-verified"). Otherwise only the woerterbuchnetz lemma link is cited. Wander's main entries came out 1867–1876; items in the "Zusätze und Ergänzungen" sections are in vol. 5 (1880) (DTA-verified for Kibitz, Geben, Einsteigen and Bock).
- **Kindness rule.** Lines about looks (bald heads), class or social standing, gender, religion or ethnicity are flagged and never proposed as game lines.

## Headline findings

1. **"Kibitz" for an onlooker at a card game is attested as Austrian by 1880, so Pfeifer's "20th century" date is too late.** Wander vol. 5 (1880): "Es ist ein Kibitz. So nennt man in Oesterreich die Zuseher beim Spiele." [LEX-S12]. Pfeifer/DWDS dates *kiebitzen* "beim Kartenspiel zusehen und Ratschläge erteilen" to the "20. Jh." and derives it from Rotwelsch *kiewischen*, with the bird name as a later association [LEX-S24]. A secondary source reports a Vienna chess-paper use in 1855 ("gaffenden Menge von Kibitzen") [LEX-S35, secondary, not verified at source]. By 1908 a Baden bei Wien Tarock club had an honorary post, the "Ehrenkibitz" [LEX-S29].
2. **Viennese Tarock had its own verb for doubling: *spritzen*.** Wander vol. 4 (1876), citing Hügel 153b: "Ich spriz den Dreier. … Beim Tarok, ich sage contra, was doppelt gilt." [LEX-S15]. A local paper, 1898: "zwischen einem gespritzten 'Dreier' und einem angesagten 'Pagat'" [LEX-S28]. For a Viennese opponent, "I spritz!" is the period word for "Kontra!".
3. **The oldest German attestations of the Sküs and the Pagat found here are from 1762** [review: note 05-S12, Bauer 1756, already has "Scüs", "Mongue" and "Pagat" in a Tarock rule book, so 1762 is only the oldest *non-rule-book* find in this fragment; the two notes should not both claim 'oldest']. Musäus, *Grandison der Zweite* (1762): "der Skies und Pakat im Tarock" [LEX-S25]. By 1796 there is a full table-talk scene: a player calls the Skis "Monsieur Skis" and wins with a "natürliche Kavallerie" against a "skissirte Kavallerie" [LEX-S26].
4. **Meyers (vol. 19, 6th ed., which cites a 1906 work) records a spoken formula and gives the etymology:** "Der Skis (richtiger Sküs, vom ital. scusa, Entschuldigung)"; and the call "»Ich skisiere (exküsiere) mich!«" [LEX-S22]. It also explains "Pagat ultimieren" and "Latons" (blank cards).
5. **A Viennese dealer's patter is recorded:** "Liegen thut's tüchtig, gegeben ist's richtig und's Ganze ist unrichtig. (Wien.) So sagt der Geber beim Kartenspiel." [LEX-S20]. Other Austrian card-table sayings from Wander: "Einsteigen wie der Frosch ins Wasser" (Lower Austria, 1880); "Die ersten Hunde ertränkt man gern" (Upper Austria, 1870: early winners lose in the end); "Gestochen der Bock, warum geht er ins Kraut" (Upper Austria, 1880, said when a trick is taken); "Der gibt wie der Schneider von Pressburg" (Lower Austria, 1880, said to someone who deals twice in a row); "Er spielt um die Sonne, ehe es Tag wird" (Vienna, 1876) [LEX-S12][LEX-S13][LEX-S18][LEX-S20][LEX-S21].
6. **A proverb ties the Fool to Tarock:** "Im Spiel (Tarok) gilt der Narr am meisten" (Wander vol. 4, 1876, after Eiselein and Simrock) [LEX-S13], and "Man muss allweg ein Narren im Spiel haben. – Lehmann … 1) Beim Tarok." (Wander vol. 3, 1873) [LEX-S14]. Both suit the moment the Sküs is played.
7. **Strohmann (DWB):** the earliest word is 1691 (Stieler). The "dummy at whist" sense is cited from Raabe (1864), Pückler (letters published 1873; the passage is about "whist à deux" with "Strohmänner") and Ebner-Eschenbach (1893) [LEX-S2]. The other senses are straw doll, scarecrow (1662) and front man (18th c., after French *homme de paille*) [LEX-S2][LEX-S24]. No "Strohmandl/Strohmandel" form turned up in DWB, Wander or DTA.

**[REVIEW - kindness rule, RB]** All lines flagged ⚑ above are correctly excluded. Additional cautions: "Mit solchen Karten kann Lehmann's Kutscher auch spielen" (the original is a class jab at coachmen; the proposed 'anyone could win' line is acceptable only as a luck remark); "Glück im Spiel, Unglück in der Liebe" (teases the player's love life - prefer the Wander variants about luck alone); "Er spielt wie Karl der Grosse" is fine (pace/score). Wander's gloss for the Meyers etymology differs by edition: 05 cites Meyers 4th ed. (1889) "von excuser", this fragment the 6th ed. "vom ital. scusa" - not a contradiction, but implementers should cite the edition.

Left out, recorded for history only (⚑):
- "A Kurt is wie a Hür" (Wander, Karte 92, Warsaw Yiddish-German): it compares cards to prostitutes.
- The Spiess 1796 "Bruder Skis" exchange. It is a social humiliation (calling a lady's brother the Fool, then "eine wirkliche Dame weit mehr sei, als eine skissirte Dame"). The card logic is usable; the barb is not.
- The Badener Zeitung "kahlen Köpfe" (bald heads).
- Wander's many devil and sin proverbs about card playing (e.g. "Das Kartenspiel ist des Teufels Betbüchlein", Kartenspiel 3): religious moralising, not banter.
- "Wer Pech hat, der ertrinkt auf trocknem Boden, sagte Hans, als der Zigeuner ertrank" (Pech 40): ethnic.

## Hunches followed

- **Kibitz, earliest card sense.** Pfeifer says 20th century, which seemed too late for a Viennese word. Wander's Austrian 1880 entry confirms the hunch, and the 1908 "Ehrenkibitz" is a bonus. A secondary source points to Vienna chess in 1855; I did not verify this at the source (ANNO search could not be scripted).
- **Viennese doubling verb.** Wander's full-text search for "Tarok" found Spritzen 2 ("Ich spriz den Dreier"). A DTA hit from Baden in 1898 corroborates it.
- **The Fool proverb.** Wander's full text gave "Im Spiel (Tarok) gilt der Narr am meisten" and Lehmann's "Narren im Spiel".
- **Earliest Sküs/Pagat.** DTA gave Musäus 1762 and the Spiess 1796 scene. The DTA "Pagat" hits in Abraham a Sancta Clara (1686) turned out to be Latin *pagat* ("pays"): a false positive, nothing.
- **Strohmandl in dialect lexica.** There is no "Strohmandl/-mandel" headword in DWB, Wander or Schmeller (OCR grep), and none in DTA. Schmeller's "Mandl" also means a stook of upright sheaves, which is a loose visual hunch for the stacked straw-man piles (unsupported). DWB's whist-dummy sense (1864) and Pückler's "whist à deux … strohmänner" are the nearest card uses found.
- **Castelli, *Wörterbuch der Mundart in Oesterreich unter der Enns* (1847).** Downloaded from archive.org, but the Fraktur OCR is unreadable, so this yielded nothing.
- **Idioticon Austriacum (1824).** The OCR is readable in one copy. It gave the Trumpf entry ("Pick ist Trumpf"; "Den hab i an Trumpf geben").
- **WBÖ.** wboe.oeaw.ac.at redirects or is blocked by Cloudflare. The PDFs found on austriaca.at were front matter, not entries, and were deleted. Nothing.
- **"Wer schreibt, der bleibt"** and **"Kiebitz, halt's Maul"**: no hits in DTA, and nothing citable on the web. Not recorded.

## Gaps / leads not followed

- ANNO (ÖNB newspapers) full text: the search is a JavaScript app and I could not find its API. The 1855 *Wiener Schachzeitung* "Kibitzen" quote and "Strohmandl" newspaper uses should be checked there.
- WBÖ / LIÖ entries for "Bagat" (B/P volume), "Excuse" and "Brandeln": not reachable.
- Castelli 1847 needs a better scan (BSB/MDZ or Google full view) to check "Tarock", "Kibitz", "Sküs" and "Pagat".
- The Google Books API returned 429 (quota exhausted), so there was no Google Books full-text dating.
- Kluge's older editions (1883–1910) for "Kiebitz": not fetched.
- Czech, Hungarian and Slovene equivalents: only DWB's Czech "barvu ponésti" (follow suit) and Wander's Czech parallels were found ("Zlé jest v karty hráti a trumfy neznati", "Kdo nehrá, ne prohrá", Čelakovský).
