# 01 - Wikipedia in all languages, and the citations followed back

Scope: Wikidata sitelinks; full-text search of ~45 language editions; the Tarock/Tarot family articles; Tapp Tarock, Kosakeln, Officers' Skat/Schafkopf, French Tarot "découverte"; the references of en/de Strohmandeln and Tapp Tarock traced to the sources; article histories and talk pages. All files are under `research/sources/01/` (subdirs `wp/`, `cites/`, `books/`, `ulmann/`, `search2/`, `pagat/`, `onb_copies/`).

## Headline findings

1. Only two editions have a Strohmandeln article: en and de (Wikidata Q15849459 has sitelinks `enwiki`, `dewiki` only) [01-S1]. Full-text search of 45 editions found no other article on it (cs, sk, hu, sl, hr, pl, it, fr: nothing) [01-S2]. Other editions only list the name: es and pt in a list of Austrian variants, de Tarock in a list, ko/en mirror "Two-player adaptations include Strohmandeln and Kosakeln" [01-S3].
2. The en article is a 2018 translation of the de stub, then expanded by one editor, Bermicourt (about 100 of 114 edits). The de article was started 30 June 2013 by an anonymous Austrian IP whose edit summary reads "Beck Fritz, Tarock komplet. Alle Spiele (Perlenreihe Nr. 640), eigenes Wissen" ("own knowledge"). Both talk pages are empty (en: only a translation banner; de: does not exist) [01-S4].
3. The en History section holds up against the primary sources: the 1805 Mößler text ("Taroc en deux", 25 cards put aside, never used), the 1829 Haas text ("Tarok-Tappen unter zwey Personen, oder das so genannte Strohmandeln"), and Dummett's "rather jejune game was ousted..." all say what Wikipedia says [01-S5, 01-S6, 01-S7]. But the famous "noble game of Piquet" sneer is in the **1829** text (p. 94), not only in 1839 as Wikipedia says; the original also adds a jibe at Mariage and "Tattel". The 1829 Spielbuch and the 1834 Spielbuch print identical wording, so the 1839 page is evidently a reprint (the 1839 volume itself not read).
4. "18 minutes/hand" in the infobox has no source. It was typed in as "12 minutes" on 15 June 2018 and changed to "18 minutes/hand" on 23 June 2018, both by the article's main editor, with no citation. Neither Furr nor tarocchino.com mentions play time. Sibling infoboxes by the same editor carry similar unsourced figures: Officers' Skat "7-8 minutes/hand", Officers' Schafkopf "15 minutes/hand", Tapp Tarock "20 min/deal" [01-S4, 01-S8]. Treat as an editor's estimate, not data.
5. Error found in en Strohmandeln: the scoring table says Rostopschin is taken "with Tarocks VII and VIII". The text originally (5 June 2020) said XVII and XVIII, matching Furr; the "1" was lost when the table was built on 16 June 2021 [01-S4, 01-S9]. Three-way conflict across sources: XVII+XVIII (Furr; en Strohmandeln before 2021) vs XVII+XVI (en and de Königrufen articles) vs VII+VIII (en Strohmandeln now).
6. The en table credits Furr with a "Trull (tricks) = 1" bonus; Furr's text has no Trull bonus at all. Furr also says of all bonuses "none of them may be announced", whereas the article's prose, following Beck, lets a player announce Trull/Royal Trull [01-S9, 01-S7].
7. The en article's "French Tarot - La Découverte uses six straw men per player with two cards in each stack" is uncited and matches neither source I could find: Furr describes 12 packets of two per player [01-S9]; the pagat.com two-player French Tarot (by Maxence Crossley) uses six piles of three, 21 in hand, no bidding [01-S11]. No Wikipedia French Tarot article (en or fr) has a découverte section [01-S2, 01-S3].
8. Ulmann: the en article cites "Das Buch der Familienspiele (1890) pp. 244-245" for the "very attractive" game. I could not fetch that book. I did read Ulmann's *Illustrirtes Wiener Tarokbuch*, 2nd ed., which has the "Strohmann-Tarok (en deux)" chapter (pp. 69-73) and states the better game's attractions in his own words; so the idea is Ulmann's, but the 1890 pages are [UNVERIFIED] [01-S10].

## 2. Authorship and history

- en Strohmandeln: created 11 June 2018 by Bermicourt as a translation ("translated page" banner: de version 175182887, i.e. the 19 March 2018 de revision) [01-S4]. The first version had only Cards, Tricks, one Wiener Zeitung link. History section appeared 10 March 2019 (1829, 1839, Dummett), Ulmann cite 11 March 2019, Mayr/Sedlaczek/Quapil 11 March 2019, "forerunner" 1800 text 21 Feb 2020, Rostopschin 5 June 2020, scoring table 16 June 2021, Découverte line 25 June 2020 [01-S4]. Last edit 5 Nov 2025 (link fix). 114 revisions total.
- de Strohmandeln: 28 revisions, 2013-2025. Rule text by anonymous Austrian IPs (86.33.x) in July 2013, "Beleg" (reference) added by user ArthurMcGill 28 July 2013, small clarifications by KnightMove 2019 and Bermicourt 2025 (image only). No Geschichte section; no sources other than Beck pp. 40-45 and the Wiener Zeitung 2007 link [01-S4].
- First de revision already says the name comes from the three packets "à vier Karten" and follows Beck; one early typo said "dreimal vier Päckchen à vier Karten" (corrected later) [01-S4].
- Sibling articles: en Tapp Tarock (166 revs, started 12 Feb 2015 by Countakeshi "translated from German wiki"; 131 by Bermicourt); de Tapp-Tarock (94 revs, started 1 Aug 2007 by an IP "Ich spiele dieses Spiel selbst in einer Altherrenrunde nach alter Überlieferung"); en Kosakeln 116 revs, 94 by Bermicourt, DYK 10 June 2019 hook "if two Austrians say they are 'playing Cossack'"; en Officers' Skat 109 revs, created 23 Feb 2018 translated from de; en Officers' Schafkopf created 11 Nov 2018 [01-S8].
- Talk pages: en Talk:Tapp Tarock has a real discussion (2019-2020) on Unterer/Oberer; Bermicourt there summarises Dummett's four stages of the Tapp talon, and Countakeshi posts that the 1821 rules had a 54-card and a 42-card game and that McLeod has found a Baden precursor (Dreierles, pagat.com/tarot/dreierles.html; "ongoing speculation" that Tapp came via Further Austria/Switzerland in the Napoleonic wars) [01-S8]. de Diskussion:Tapp-Tarock: 2007 complaint that the rules are "unbefriedigend", 2013 note that the article shows only a fraction of the Austrian three-player variants (points to Beck, Mayr/Sedlaczek) [01-S8]. de Diskussion:Offiziersskat records deal variants. Nothing on Strohmandeln anywhere.

## 3. Following each citation back (en Strohmandeln)

Claim by claim: what Wikipedia says / what the source says.

- **1800 Neuestes Spielbuch pp. 145-146, "Taroc en deux", 25-card dummy never used.** Verified, but the edition I read is Vienna **1805** (Mößler), pp. 145-146; Google Books lists a 1802 edition, 1799/1800 records also exist; Dummett cites the 1802 Vienna printing as following the Berlin 1800 account. Text: "Vom Taroc en deux. Das Taroc en deux wird von zwei Personen gespielt ... Der Geber giebt zu 5 Karten, seinem Gegner 25, sich selbst 28 Karten; die übrigen 25 Karten werden bei Seite gelegt: und gar nicht zum Spiele gerechnet" ("Taroc en deux is played by two persons ... the dealer deals in fives ['giebt zu 5 Karten'], 25 to his opponent, 28 to himself; the remaining 25 cards are laid aside and not counted in the game at all") [REVIEW RA: the earlier translation "dealer gives 5 cards, his opponent 25, himself 28" is wrong: 5+25+28+25 = 83, not 78. "zu 5 Karten" means dealing in packets of five; the dealer's 28 = 25 + 3 for the Scat, per the next sentence of the source, "Der Geber legt die sich mehr gegebenen 3 Karten in den Scat"]. It is a 78-card game scored like Piquet ("nach Partie bis 100 Points"), Pagat 15 in the middle / 25 ultimo, so it is not an ancestor of Strohmandeln in any rules sense; the shared name element is only "en deux" (cf. Ulmann's chapter title "Strohmann-Tarok (en deux)") [01-S5].
- **1829 Neuestes allgemeines Spielbuch p. 94.** Verified. Heading: "E. Tarok-Tappen unter zwey Personen, oder das so genannte Strohmandeln." Text: "Dieses Spiel wird ganz so wie das gewöhnliche Tappen unter drey Personen gespielt, auch werden die Karten wie für drey Theilnehmer ausgegeben, nachdem die obersten sechs Blätter als Kauftalon gelegt sind. - Die dritte fehlende Person ist der Strohmann, dessen Karten verdeckt ... liegen bleiben. Während nun die andern beyden mitsammen spielen, wirft sich derjenige, welchem der Stich gehört, allemahl das oberste Blatt von den Karten den Strohmanns zu, das jedoch, wäre es auch der Scüs, nicht sticht, sondern nur dem Empfänger des Stiches zu dessen Vortheil zählt." ("This game is played exactly like ordinary Tappen among three persons ... the third, missing person is the Strohmann, whose cards stay face down ... the winner of each trick throws himself the top card of the straw man's cards, which, even if it is the Sküs, does not trump but only counts for the receiver's benefit.") Wikipedia's "Skartindels" is **not** in the source: the original says "nur Taroks oder leere Blätter" ("only tarocks or empty cards") [01-S6].
- **1839 p. 121 ("noble game of Piquet").** Not read directly. The identical sentence is at 1829 p. 94: "Indeß da hierbey weit weniger die Geschicklichkeit als der blinde Zufall wirkt, möchte diese Unterhaltung wohl nur von Leuten gewählt werden, denen das edle Piquetspiel gänzlich unbekannt ist, zumahl da es in Einseitigkeit noch das veraltete Mariage und jene neuere Mißgeburt 'Tatteln' benannt, bey weitem übertrifft." ("Since here blind chance works far more than skill, this pastime would probably be chosen only by people to whom the noble game of Piquet is entirely unknown, the more so since it far surpasses, in one-sidedness, even the antiquated Mariage and that newer abortion called 'Tatteln'.") Translation of "Einseitigkeit" is uncertain; the clause is a three-way put-down, in which Strohmandeln is the thing that "surpasses" (übertrifft) Mariage and Tattel in one-sidedness, i.e. the book is calling it one-sided. The same words appear in the 1834 Spielbuch [01-S6, 01-S6b]. The sneer is therefore c. 1829 at the latest, not an 1839 judgment. Wikipedia's paraphrase "because the game depends more on luck than skill" is accurate.
- **Dummett 1980 pp. 457-459.** Verified in the archive.org OCR. He says the 1829 account is "a cursory description of an adaptation of Tarok-Tappen for two players", reprinted in Alvensleben's *Encyclopädie der Spiele*, Anton's, and Stabenow 1908-10; in the early form the dealer deals three hands of 16 after a six-card talon, the second hand is a Strohmann "left in a pile face down", and "The winner of each trick takes the top card of the dummy hand, shows it to his opponent and adds it to his tricks." Then: "This rather jejune game was ousted by a much better and quite different one, also called Strohmandeln, Strohmandl, Strohmann-Tarock or, in Hungary, Strohmandli. It does not seem ever to have been known in Germany, but only in Austria and Hungary, and must date from the second half of the nineteenth century, the 1880s at the latest, but perhaps much earlier" [01-S7]. Two differences from Wikipedia: Dummett gives "1880s at the latest, perhaps much earlier" and "described in both of Ulmann's books", not "by no later than 1890"; and the en article's phrase that Dummett's "much better" game is "a genuine two-player game" is Wikipedia's gloss. Dummett does not use the word "attractive"; that is attributed to Ulmann (see below).
- **Ulmann 1890, pp. 244-245.** Not fetched [UNVERIFIED]. Ulmann's *Illustrirtes Wiener Tarokbuch* 2nd ed. (Hartleben, undated, c. 1899-1900) pp. 68-73, which I read as page images, says: "Das Spiel wird durch den Strohmann viel anziehender" ("the game becomes much more attractive through the straw man") [REVIEW RA: on p. 69 (read in the page image) this sentence closes the Neunzehnerrufen chapter and refers to Neunzehnerrufen en deux ("das Neunzehnerrufen en deux, bei welchem zwei Strohmänner aufliegen, bietet ... weitaus höheres Interesse"); it is NOT said of Strohmann-Tarok, which begins in the next paragraph (Cap. VI). Do not cite it for the Strohmandeln game], "Strohmann-Tarok (en deux): ... bei welcher 54 Blätter zur Verwendung kommen; dieselbe ist eine der schwierigsten, aber auch interessantesten und erfreut sich mit Recht allgemeiner Beliebtheit" ("one of the most difficult, but also most interesting, and rightly enjoys general popularity"); "Dass nur zwei Personen spielen, ist in Vieler Augen ein Vorzug; man ist hier nicht von der Mithilfe eines Partners abhängig ... stets selbst seines eigenen Glückes Schmied" ("that only two play is in many eyes an advantage ... each the smith of his own fortune"). Rules there: deal 30 cards in six throws of five, "Vorhand" declares "aufnehmen"; six packets of four cards laid after the bid; top card of each packet turned, Tarock or King set aside and taken into the hand after the opponent has seen it, other suit cards stay as face-up top cards; aufnehmer needs 36 (35 2/3) points, wins 2, loses 4; Pagat ultimo worth 2 or 1; "Trull" and the four Kings give "eine Prämie von einem Point, müssen aber angesagt werden" (must be announced) [01-S10]. The preface to the first edition says the book fills a gap because "das Strohmann-Tarok en deux, das Paskiewitsch, das Neunzehnerrufen mit Strohmann" had never been described [01-S10]. This phrase is a lead (a Neunzehnerrufen with a straw man existed).
- **Beck 1972 pp. 40-45; Bamberger 2011 pp. 23-28; Mayr & Sedlaczek 2008 p. 380; Löw 1956.** Not fetched (books). Dummett's digest (read) records divergences between Beck, Löw, Babsch, Werner, Unger, Ulmann, Widder Lajos, Piatnik [01-S7].
- **Furr 2009, "Straw Man Tarock" (archive.org tarocchi-philebus; infobox cite "p. 109").** Verified in the archive.org OCR. The page labelled 108 begins the section; the quote "I'm not a fan of two player games generally and you won't find many here. However, this one is rather fun and well worth learning for when you're short a player or for those occasions when three would be a crowd" is on the page headed 108, so the infobox "p. 109" is off by one. [REVIEW RA: the OCR does not show whether the printed page numbers are headers or footers, so the off-by-one claim is UNVERIFIED] Furr's rules: 54-card pack, three packets of five and three straw-man piles of four, "Eldest" may bid "I'll Play"; both pass: hand worth 2; declarer 3 if won, 4 for the defender if lost; "Straw Men": top card of each pile turned; King or trump taken into hand; "Players may not take a card into their hand until their opponent has seen it"; "All 27 tricks"; bonuses "none of them may be announced": With 45 scores 4; With 26 or less 5 for the defender; Slam 12; "The Birds: All score 1 point"; Pagat Ultimo; "An Owl" II; "A Cockatoo" III; "Rostopschin: ... consecutive tricks with the XVII and the XVIII of trumps ... call 'Ros' when playing the XVII ... 'Topschin' when playing the XVIII. Scores 1 point". No Quapil, no Trull bonus. The en table's "Furr: Trull (tricks) 1" is unsupported; the "Simple win (both passed) 2" fits. Furr also says in the intro that his sources are Dummett/McLeod and the pagat site [01-S9].
- **tarocchino.com archived page (Wayback 10 Feb 2010, page33.html).** Fetched. It is word for word the same text as Furr (Furr's book was the web page first; the web page is headed "StrawMan / Straw Man Tarock"), with the same XVII/XVIII Rostopschin. Not an independent source [01-S9b].
- **Wiener Zeitung 25 Sept 2007, Mayr & Sedlaczek "Wie sehen die idealen Regeln aus" (2 pages; update 16 June 2011).** Fetched from tagblatt-wienerzeitung.at. Verified: "Das Tarock für 2 Spieler wird auch Strohmandeln oder Strohmanntarock genannt ... Strohmandeln wird heute nicht nur in Österreich, sondern auch in Ungarn gespielt. Dort heißt es Strohmandli." Calls: "Ich nehme auf!", "Weiter!", "Ich passe!". Scores in cents: simple game 20 c either way, aufgenommen won 30 c, lost 40 c (= 3/4/2 as Wikipedia has it). Win condition is "mindestens 35 Punkte und 2 Blatt" (35 points and 2 Blatt, i.e. 35 2/3), so "36 points" is the same thing; a 35:35 draw also covers 34 pts/2 Blatt vs 35/1 Blatt; "Nullrunde" doubles the next round of 2 games. Premiums "nur ... still" (only quiet), no announced Pagat ("weil der Erfolg ... davon abhinge, ob in den letzten Strohmännerkarten des Gegners Tarock liegen"); Uhu, Kakadu, maybe Quapil each 10 c; Trull in tricks and four Kings in tricks 10 c quiet; quiet Valat 120 c; no Kontra ("weil das Spiel durch Aufdecken der Strohmännerkarten oft eine überraschende Wendung nimmt"). No Rostopschin, no major-win bonuses (Wikipedia table correct on that). Wikipedia's "Quapil" and Pagat-"announced" rows from M&S: M&S say no announcements, which conflicts with the Beck line [01-S12].
- **Löw 1956 pp. 102-110 cited for Kosakeln;** Dummett p. 488 for Kosakeln as adaptation of Illustrated Tarock: not verified (Dummett chapter 24 in part 4 contains the word "Kosakeln" at one line, 6670 of the OCR, not read in context) [01-S7].

## 4. Contradictions between editions and between article and sources

- en vs de, deal: en says forehand has the right to decide first and, if both pass, "they can agree to play regardless, but at a lower game value"; de says the hands are thrown in and re-dealt by old forehand ("Es kann vereinbart werden, in diesem Falle dennoch, allerdings zum halben Spielwert, zu spielen"). Both come from Beck, so the two readings are both in the tradition. Dummett: "There are two alternative rules for what happens if both pass" [01-S4, 01-S7].
- de says 2 "Werteinheiten" for a played game, 1 if not played, 2 for Trull announcement, 2 for Tarock I ultimo, 2 for the Mond caught by the Sküs; en table "Beck" column gives 1, 2/3 (declarer win 2, defender win 3), Trull 1, Pagat 1; the Mondfang is "1 game point" in en prose. So the two Wikipedia editions give different Beck values for Trull, Pagat and Mondfang (2/2/2 in de vs 1/1/1 in en). Dummett gives 1 game point for each of Trull and Pagat ultimo (Beck "some award 2 or even 3") [01-S4, 01-S7].
- de says the last card of a pile goes to the hand "ohne Aufdecken" (unrevealed); en repeats that. Dummett: Beck and Babsch agree (taken into hand even if not King/Tarock), Werner says it stays face up until played [01-S7].
- Rostopschin: VII/VIII (en Strohmandeln, since June 2021) vs XVII/XVIII (Furr, the same article until then) vs XVII/XVI (en and de Königrufen "an old bonus") [01-S4, 01-S9, 01-S3].
- Win threshold: "36 of 70" (de, en, Dummett, Furr) vs "35 points and 2 Blatt" (Mayr/Sedlaczek) vs "36 (35 2/3)" (Ulmann); these are the same rule counted two ways [01-S10, 01-S12].
- Pagat announced: de/en (Beck) allow announcing Trull; Dummett says "There is no announcement of Pagat ultimo"; Furr "none of them may be announced"; M&S "nur still" (quiet only) [01-S7, 01-S9, 01-S12].
- Dating: en says the 54-card game "first appeared in 1829"; Dummett's phrase is that the 1829 and later Austrian books describe "an adaptation of Tarok-Tappen for two players"; not in the 1821 Anweisung nor in the German Neuestes Spiel-Taschenbuch of 1830 [01-S7].
- Tapp Tarock: en says "the introduction of the Pagat Ultimo, according to John McLeod, is believed to have come from ... Trappola". Dummett p. 438 says the opposite for the bonus: "We saw that the Pagat ultimo bonus was probably not connected with Trappola; but it has been very plausibly conjectured by Mr John McLeod that the introduction of an announcement of intention to gain the bonus was inspired by that game", via Hundertspiel. The en article conflates the bonus with the announcement [01-S7].
- Two-player French Tarot: three incompatible descriptions (see headline 7) [01-S9, 01-S11].
- Kosakeln: en Strohmandeln says it "was introduced in the 1950s"; en Kosakeln says it is first recorded as "Kosaken" by Löw 1956, but that Illustrated Tarock itself appears in the 1950s (Löw 1954); "first recorded in" is not the same as "introduced" [01-S3].

## 5. Hunches followed

- "18 minutes/hand": see headline 4. Yielded: unsourced editor estimate; the only article on Wikipedia with that string (search returned 1 hit).
- Where the "Strohmandli" in Hungary comes from: Dummett and M&S both say "Strohmandli"; Dummett also lists "Widder Lajos" as a Hungarian source on the modern game [01-S7, 01-S12]. Nothing found on the Hungarian or Czech Wikipedias.
- Why "Neuestes Spielbuch 1800" while Google Books and Dummett say 1802/1805: three printings share the text; the OCR copy I read is 1805 (Mößler's own preface not read) [01-S5].
- The word "Strohmandel" turns up in 19th-century humour weeklies in a Google Books feed search (Figaro 1888, Kikeriki 1893, Wiener Leben 1890, Neue Fliegende 1886, "Die Frau Hausmeisterin" 1886, "Die lieben Kinder" 1898). I did not open them (Google Books is blocking page access; snippets not obtainable). Passed to other strands as leads [01-S13].
- Ulmann's phrase "Neunzehnerrufen mit Strohmann" and "Tarok en doux" (sic) in the 1st-edition preface; "Strohmann-Tarok (en deux)" as the chapter title ties the modern game's name to the older Taroc en deux [01-S10].
