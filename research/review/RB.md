## 2. Quote match statistics

Method: every quoted fragment of 8+ characters (straight quotes or „ “) was extracted from each note; normalised (lower case, ſ and ß to s, umlauts folded, all non-alphanumerics dropped, ANNO `<br/>` removed) and searched as a substring in all saved text under `research/sources/` (979 text-like files plus PDFs without a text twin; 80 M characters). Fragments split on "..." / "[...]" / "/" were required to match in every part. Non-matches got an anchored fuzzy score (difflib on 10-gram anchors; "near" = 0.80-0.999, OCR-spelling-level differences).

## 5. Period-date claims for the bird names (Uhu etc.)

- 05 states "Uhu first appears in Hungary 1902 in Alsós; entered Austrian Tarock in the 20th century (first for Tarock II in Unger 1937)" and marks it [WP]: it is a faithful report of en.wikipedia (`web/wp_en_koenigrufen.txt` l. 93-97) but second-hand; the research behind it (the wiki's `Owl` reference) was not read by anyone. Status: **[WP second-hand, unverified at source]**.
- "Hügel 1873 and Schranka 1905 do not have them as card terms": verified (Hügel has "Kakadu" = a hairstyle over the forehead, l. 11232; "Buhu/Uhu" only as the owl, l. 5405; Schranka none).
- ANNO probe: conjunctive searches (Uhu + Pagat + Kakadu etc.) returned only noise (racehorses "Kakadu I.", Schnitzler's "Der grüne Kakadu", "Uhu" in advertisements). Quoted-phrase and hyphenated queries ("Pagat Uhu", "Pagat-Uhu", "Uhu-Ultimo", "Uhu ultimo") returned 0 hits, which may be a genuine absence or a limit of the search syntax, so **no counter-evidence was found, and no negative is claimed**. The en.wikipedia page also shows card images captioned "TarockUhu1890.png" / "TarockUhuAdler1880.png"; these are modern file names for the card II (eagle owl), not period terms, and nothing in the note relies on them.
- Mayr & Sedlaczek 2007 (WZ, `07d/wz2.html.txt`) use "Uhu, Kakadu und ev. auch Quapil" for Strohmandeln, so the birds are modern-Strohmandeln vocabulary; period (Werner-era) Strohmandl has no birds. Consistent with 05's advice.

## 9. New findings (leads, not put into the notes)

- Hügel 1873 has "Mariasch, ein in Oesterreich beliebtes Kartenspiel zu Zweien" (`05/dict/huegel1873_djvu.txt` l. 13978): a second two-hand game named in an 1873 Viennese dialect dictionary; it is Marjage, not Strohmandl, but relevant to the "game for two" picture (note 06 or 10 may want it). Hügel and Schranka have no Strohmandl entry (grep "strohm": only Strohmatte).
- The 1892 Kohn sketch gives period slang evidence that "Kosak" meant a sharper / cheating partner (plays "Halbpart ... Zeichen machen"), which also decodes the 1880 Pester Lloyd "Opfer der Kosaken" and may link to the game name Kosakeln (note 06 territory); not followed.
- Werner (p.133): "Auch beim Strohmanndl kann Pagat-Ultimo gemacht werden und wird mit 1 Point, eventuell noch mit einer Extraprämie honorirt" - the Pagat ultimo is paid unannounced; combined with "kann natürlich nie ultimo angesagt werden" this is the cleanest period rule for the rules menu (already in 04/06 in part).
- M&S "4 von 10" reconciles exactly with a conditional probability (item 25): worth stating in the strategy tutor ("the King sleeps in about 40 % of cases when you do not hold it").
- Modern Nestroy text (Reclam 1996, PG) differs from the 1890 Bonz text at several Tarock lines (item 14); a banter writer who wants period-authentic Nestroy should quote the PG/Reclam text, which also uses "tous les trois" for the Trull in the couplet.
- Pfeifer's "kiebitzen (20. Jh.)" is contradicted by 05b/04 (Wander 1880, ANNO 1880/1895); a good small correction for the glossary.
