# 02 - Modern rule descriptions of Strohmandeln, compared clause by clause

Strand 02. All claims cite sources fetched and read in this session; local copies are in
`research/sources/02/` and `research/sources/02b/` (paths given in the Sources list).
"Modern" here = c. 1890 to the present (the "later form"). Scoring units differ by source
(game points vs. cents); I normalise 10 cents = 1 game point only where the source itself does.

## Headline findings

1. **pagat.com has no Strohmandeln page.** The Austria national-games page merely lists
   "Strohmandeln (2 players)" as "less widespread" [02-S9]; the Droggn page names it among
   eastern-Austrian games [02-S9]; Tapp Tarock page links only to Kruepl and Wikipedia [02-S9].
   The sitemap contains no Strohmandeln entry (grep of the saved sitemap) [02-S9]. So McLeod's
   authority reaches us only through Dummett & McLeod, whose Vol. 2 is cited by pagat [02-S9];
   I could not read that volume. The earlier Dummett (1980) text *is* read [02-S1] and is the
   best neutral digest: it surveys Ulmann, Bermann, Werner, Unger, Beck, Loew, Babsch,
   Widder Lajos and the Piatnik leaflet and says "these various descriptions agree very closely".
2. **Core mechanics are stable across 120 years**: 54 cards; 15 hand cards (3 x 5) plus three
   4-card face-down piles per player; "Ich nehme auf" is the only positive bid; tarocks and
   kings are taken from the pile tops into the hand, other cards stay face up and play as
   part of the hand; 27 tricks; 36 of 70 wins [02-S1][02-S2][02-S5].
3. **The one real mechanical split is the LAST (4th) card of each pile.** Beck, Babsch, Mayr & Sedlaczek:
   it goes straight into the hand, whatever it is [02-S1][02-S2]. Werner (c. 1900s): a last card that
   "sich nicht zur Aufnahme in die Handkarte eignet" (is not a tarock/king) stays face up until played
   [02-S5]. **[REVIEW RA correction]** The earlier text here said Werner leaves the last card face up "even
   if a king/tarock". That is Dummett's report of Werner ([02-S1]: "remains face up on the table until it is
   played, even if it is a King or a Tarock"), but Werner's own words (p. 133, 'Auch das letzte Blatt eines
   jeden Talon-Päckchens muß, wenn es sich nicht zur Aufnahme in die Handkarte eignet, so lange offen
   liegen bleiben, bis es gelegentlich gespielt wird') carry the condition "if it is unsuitable for taking
   into the hand", so on Werner's text a last-card king/tarock IS taken. Dummett's gloss and Werner's text
   disagree; the real split is Beck/Babsch/M&S (take any last card) vs Werner (a low last card stays up). Dummett: with good memories it makes
   no difference [02-S1] (but see ambiguity A3: it does change who may be compelled to
   follow suit or trump from it).
4. **Wikipedia's Strohmandeln table contains errors against its own cited sources.** It
   gives Rostopschin as "VII and VIII"; Furr (its cited source) says XVII and XVIII
   [02-S3][02-S4]; the Torberg quotation as printed in en/de Wikipedia Königrufen also says XVII and XVIII
   but is second-hand ([02-S8]; Torberg's book never seen by this note nor by RA; the same Wikipedia
   articles' glossary says XVII and XVI). It credits Furr with "Trull in tricks 1"; Furr's page lists no
   Trull or Kings bonus at all [02-S3]. Its "Mondfang" option for Strohmandeln appears in
   no primary description I could read (Mondfang exists only in the Koenigrufen sources)
   [02-S6][02-S9]. Treat the WP page as a pointer, not a source.
5. **The Wiener Zeitung 2007 article is the fullest modern text** (Mayr & Sedlaczek). It has
   both pages (page 2 recovered from web.archive.org) with the mechanics, the redeal advice,
   a "Nullrunde" doubling rule and a complete price list in cents [02-S2].
6. **Rostopschin is, per Wikipedia's quotation of Torberg (second-hand, [UNVERIFIED — review]), a
   Cafe Central bonus** (two successive tricks with XVII then XVIII, calling "Ross!" then "Topschin!");
   Torberg himself, as quoted, calls Rostopschin the name of a whole Königrufen variant, and only Sedlaczek
   (also second-hand via Wikipedia) says it is a bonus name. Why it is named after the governor of Moscow:
   no source I found says. See Hunches.
7. **No source gives a match target or game length.** Sources only give per-deal game
   points/cents, a doubling after a tie, and (M&S) a "Runde" of two deals. A target score is
   therefore a design choice (see section 4).

## 2. Ambiguities a programmer will hit (with the evidence each way)

**A1. Can a player lead from a straw man?** Yes. W: the player may use the open talon cards "zum Stechen, Ausspielen und Zugeben" [02-S5]; MS: "ausspielen, stechen oder zugeben" [02-S2]; D: "play any of them to a trick" [02-S1]. When the hand is empty the player "nur mehr von den Strohmaennern zu Ende spielt bzw. spielen muss" [02-S2].

**A2. Must you play a straw-man card of the led suit if your hand cannot follow, and may you play it when your hand *can* follow?** W: a player without the suit in hand "aus dem Talon bedienen muss, falls sich unter dessen Deckkarten ein Blatt dieser Farbe findet" [02-S5]; MS says the same [02-S2]. Neither source forbids playing the exposed card when the hand also holds the suit; D's "part of his hand" implies the union of hand and exposed cards is the legal set [02-S1]. DE-WP's "und umgekehrt" is ambiguous [02-S7]. Same question applies to trumping: after failing to follow suit, trump must be played from hand or exposed cards (D: "or to trump a card in a suit in which he is void") [02-S1].

**A3. Can a straw man ever show a trump or king?** Under the "taken immediately" rule, no: every exposed K/tarock leaves the pile at once [02-S1][02-S2]. Under Dummett's reading of Werner's last-card rule a final-card tarock or king can lie face up [02-S1] (but Werner's own wording does not say this, see headline 3); then it could be forced as a follower/trumper. This is the only way the rule difference could change legality; D thinks players with good memories won't notice [02-S1].

**A4. Is the last card of a pile shown to the opponent?** WP says no ("without being revealed") [02-S6]; MS says it is "sofort aufgenommen" [02-S2]; D says "as soon as it is exposed" [02-S1]. The first reading hides information (hidden card in the hand); the other two reveal it. Unresolved from the sources read; Beck not seen.

**A5. When exactly does the replacement flip happen, and who does it?** W: "sogleich nach Umlegung des betreffenden Stiches" [02-S5]; MS: "sofort nach Einziehen des betreffenden Stiches" [02-S2]. So within a trick the card you used leaves a hole you cannot refill, and the follower cannot take advantage of what the leader's flip reveals. If both players used a straw card in the same trick, flips happen for both (order unspecified).

**A6. The chain after a flip**: if the new card is a tarock/king it is taken and the next is turned, until a low suit card or the pile ends [02-S1][02-S2][02-S5]. W adds that a player holding only tarocks and kings in all three piles takes the whole talon and one with only low suit cards may take nothing [02-S5].

**A7. Order of the initial flipping.** Left-to-right per pile, each pile fully chained before the next (WP-Beck, MS) [02-S6][02-S2]; W says "der Reihe nach" [02-S5]. Which player flips first is not specified in any source; likely irrelevant as they flip their own piles. Opponent must see each card before it is taken [02-S2][02-S3][02-S1] - in software, show it and delay the take.

**A8. Hand-size limits.** None given. Hand starts at 15, can grow up to 27 (if all piles taken) and can shrink to 0. Every player plays exactly 27 cards in 27 tricks [02-S1][02-S3]. There is no discarding in this game (contrast Tapp-Tarock).

**A9. Is the opponent allowed to see the set-aside cards?** Yes, must see before they are taken [02-S1][02-S2][02-S3]; MS recommend laying them openly aside first [02-S2]. So the *hand* grows with publicly known cards; only the unknown part is the 15 original cards plus unflipped pile cards.

**A10. Lead after both-pass, and with a bidder.** Dealer's opponent in all but Loew; Loew: bidder leads [02-S1]; Furr same as Loew ("If both players passed, then Dealer's opponent leads... Otherwise it is Declarer") [02-S3]. This differs only when the dealer bid, which is when the leader is the dealer.

**A11. Both pass.** Redeal or play a simple game [02-S1][02-S2][02-S3][02-S6]. Menu choice; if redeal, deal piles only after bidding [02-S2].

**A12. Ties.** Which hands can tie: simple game only (MS); under a bid 35 is a loss [02-S1][02-S2]. Effect of a tie: doubles next deal (W, WP-Beck), next *round* of two deals (MS), or following round (D) [02-S5][02-S2][02-S1].

**A13. Does Pagat count if it is played in the last trick but the hand's *last* card is a straw-man card?** W warns that Pagat ultimo cannot be announced because the opponent's last straw card could be a tarock; and advises hoarding pile cards to keep Pagat ultimo threats alive [02-S5]. Programmers must track that "last trick" is trick 27, regardless of whether the card comes from hand or pile. The sources do not say whether the Pagat must be played from the hand or may come from a pile (it could only be a face-up pile card under Dummett's reading of W's last-card rule, since a Pagat is always a tarock and taken otherwise; on Werner's own wording a last-card Pagat would be taken) [02-S5][02-S1] [REVIEW RA: qualified, see headline 3].

**A14. Do bonuses require the winner of the specific trick, and who gets points on a loss?** W: loss of the Pagat in the last trick costs "die gleiche Ersatzstrafe" [02-S5]; D: opponent scores 1 [02-S1]. Uhu/Kakadu: WP's note that these payments go to the opponent if the card is beaten [02-S6]; MS price them like Pagat [02-S2].

**A15. Are held honours (Trull/4 Kings) paid on declaration or only in tricks?** Declared-in-hand (W, D, Beck: 1 pt, even if never won) versus quiet-in-tricks (MS: 10 c) [02-S5][02-S1][02-S2][02-S6]. For F, no such bonus.

**A16. Rostopschin timing**: both calls ("Ros", "Topschin") must be said while the card is played, and the two tricks must be consecutive: F [02-S3]; Torberg: "sonst galt's nicht" [02-S8].

**A17. Cards with a point value of 0.** M&S "35 Punkte und 2 Blatt" arithmetic: 70 total, odd 2 cards count 1, odd 1 counts 0; so a player's tally in thirds must be rounded [02-S1][02-S2]. D notes that "24 points and 2 cards" is the usual way to speak [02-S1].

**A18. Contra / raising.** Only Loew allows a Kontra (dealer on a bid); no Rekontra [02-S1]; MS recommend none [02-S2].

## 4. Recommended canonical ruleset

Chosen so that each default has a named source and each divergence becomes a menu toggle.

1. **Pack and ranks**: 54-card *Industrie und Glueck*; Skis highest trump, XXI-I, suits K Q C V + 10 9 8 7 (black) or A 2 3 4 (red); card values 5/4/3/2/1 [02-S1][02-S3].
2. **Counting**: in threes minus two; odd 2 cards = 1, odd 1 = 0; total 70; win with 36 [02-S1][02-S3].
3. **Deal**: lot for first dealer, then alternate [02-S1][02-S3][02-S5]. 3 x 5 to each, then six piles of 4 alternately, Vorhand first [02-S5][02-S2]. Piles dealt after bidding (menu toggle, no effect) [02-S2].
4. **Void-hand redeal** (no tarock in the 15): offer the player the right to throw in; same dealer redeals [02-S1][02-S2]. Default on, because D says it is the Austro-Hungarian rule.
5. **Bidding**: Vorhand "Ich nehme auf" or "Weiter" / "Ich passe"; dealer then same; no Kontra by default (MS), Loew's Kontra as an option [02-S2][02-S1].
6. **Both pass**: default = play a simple game (the majority of the sources read: W, MS, F, Loew, Piatnik) [02-S5][02-S2][02-S3][02-S1]; option = redeal (Beck/Bamberger via WP) [02-S6].
7. **Straw-man flip**: left to right; chain K/tarock into hand; stops at low suit card [02-S2][02-S3][02-S1]. Opponent must see each card before it is taken [02-S1][02-S2]. Default for the last card: **taken as soon as it is exposed** (Beck, Babsch, MS), toggle "Werner: a low last card stays face up" [02-S1][02-S2][02-S5] [REVIEW RA: toggle reworded; "even if K/Tarock" is only Dummett's gloss].
8. **Exposed cards**: part of the hand; may lead, follow, trump; follow-suit obligation is over hand plus exposed cards (A2) [02-S1][02-S2][02-S5]. Flip the replacement after the trick is collected [02-S2][02-S5].
9. **Trick play**: Farbzwang and Tarockzwang, no Stichzwang; if neither suit nor trump, any card [02-S3][02-S6]. Vorhand (dealer's opponent) leads the first trick [02-S1][02-S5][02-S2]; winner leads subsequently. Option: bidder leads (Loew, Furr) [02-S1][02-S3].
10. **Values** (default = Werner/MS/Furr, which agree): simple game 2; declarer wins 3; declarer loses 4 to the opponent [02-S5][02-S2][02-S3]. Menu: Dummett/Beck low scale 1/2/3 [02-S1][02-S6]. Menu: Furr big-win bonus (declarer >=45 scores 4... defender >=44 scores 5) [02-S3].
11. **Ties**: 35:35 in a simple game = draw; next deal counts double (W, Beck) [02-S5][02-S6]; MS "Runde of two deals" as toggle [02-S2]. Under a bid, 35 loses for the declarer [02-S1].
12. **Bonuses, quiet only** (Pagat, Uhu, Kakadu; Quapil optional) at 1 point each, with the loss going to the opponent [02-S2][02-S3][02-S5][02-S1]. Trull and Four Kings: toggle between "in hand, declared" (W/D/Beck, 1 each) and "in tricks, quiet" (MS) [02-S5][02-S1][02-S2]. Rostopschin: a Furr-style fun toggle with speech lines "Ros!" / "Topschin!" [02-S3]. No announced Pagat [02-S5][02-S2][02-S1]. Mondfang off, since no primary source includes it [02-S6].
13. **Valat**: 12 points, quiet [02-S5][02-S3][02-S1].
14. **Game length/target**: sources give none; recommended design choice: first to 15 net points, or a fixed "Runde" of two deals (MS) accumulated over an evening; mark as [DESIGN, UNSOURCED]. Offer a "Nullrunde" doubling [02-S2].
15. **Information shown by the UI**: opponent's exposed pile cards face up; the cards each side set aside shown before they enter the hand; counts of unseen pile cards [02-S1][02-S2].

## 5. Hunches followed

- **Rostopschin.** Furr/tarocchino.com: "a fun bonus for taking consecutive tricks with the XVII and the XVIII", calling "Ros" then "Topschin" [02-S3][02-S4]. English and German Wikipedia both quote Torberg, *Die Tante Jolesch* (1975), p. 217: Cafe Central masters "hatten eine unglaublich komplizierte Abart des ohnehin anspruchsvollen 'Koenigrufens' erfunden, die sie 'Rostopschin' nannten", with the bonus for two successive tricks with XVII and XVIII, called "Ross!" and "Topschin!", "sonst galt's nicht"; and Sedlaczek (Mayr/Sedlaczek, *Das grosse Tarockbuch*, pp. 255 ff., as cited by WP) thinks Torberg wrongly applied the name to the whole game rather than the bonus [02-S8]. WP's Koenigrufen list gives "XVII and XVI" in one place - a third variant [02-S8]. I could not read Torberg's book (the archive.org full-text page returned nothing). The man: Fyodor Rostopchin was Governor-General of Moscow in 1812, who evacuated the city and left a detachment "charged with burning his house and the city", denied it in 1823 and later admitted it [02-S10; WebFetch summary of en.wikipedia, not saved locally]. **Why the card game uses his name: no source found.** The call "Ross! / Topschin!" is clearly a split of the surname used as a mnemonic shout; the connection with Moscow's burning (a big *Brand* for the opponent?) is conjecture [UNVERIFIED]. Next lead: Mayr/Sedlaczek, *Das grosse Tarockbuch* pp. 255 f., and Torberg p. 217 (the Wikipedia Koenigrufen page cites archive.org dietantejolescho00torb page 217, borrowable).
- **Pagat site.** Searched the sitemap and fetched the Tarot index/Austria page: pagat has no rules page for Strohmandeln [02-S9].
- **Piatnik leaflet / Loew / Babsch.** Dummett cites them [02-S1]; no online copy found (the web search did not find a Piatnik pdf).
- **Burgstaller, *Tarock zu zweit* (2017, Buchschmiede; TOC chapter "Zweiertarock oder Strohmandeln").** Only the reading sample/TOC exist online; my download of the sample PDF was truncated and unreadable (not usable). Nothing yielded.
- **Hungarian, Slovene, Czech, Slovak.** Searches in Hungarian and English/Czech/Slovene produced nothing beyond Wikipedia, Mikko's game blog and the Wienbibliothek PDF. The only Hungarian pointer is Dummett's "Strohmandli", "Widder Lajos" and MS's "Strohmandli" [02-S1][02-S2]; searches did not locate a Hungarian text.
- **Mikko's Game Blog (2008, UGC)** reports a play session; the text I downloaded did not contain a rules paragraph I could extract; not cited.
